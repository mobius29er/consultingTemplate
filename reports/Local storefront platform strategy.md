# Sell done-for-you sites from one engine

Run this as a remote-first, done-for-you subscription studio on one reusable engine, and let the family auto parts store fund the engine while local presence sites fund the family. The business makes sense and a solo founder can carry it, with a specific shape: the **2025 US median household income of $87,460** ([Census via AP/Fox 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/americans-household-income-higher-data)) converts, at an assumed 1.25x gross-up for self-employment tax and family health insurance, to roughly **$9,100 of monthly recurring gross profit**, which is **62 managed sites at $149 a month, or 17–26 sites plus two $40k custom builds a year**, reachable in a modelled 12–18 months ([viability note](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/viability_solo_founder.md)). Pursue trades and property services first, because they are the only types with type-level survey evidence that **40–57% have no website** ([PM Magazine](https://pmmag.com/articles/102211-housecall-pro-helps-home-service-companies-build-free-professional-websites); [Home Service Base](https://homeservicebase.com/reports/house-cleaning-us)), and independent auto repair (estimated 30–40% without a site, no type survey) and barbers or salons (estimated 50%+ without their own site; hair salons showed the lowest website adoption of any type, 62.6%, in a 91,904-site vendor census) next, because the presence-and-services preset is a complete product for all three on day one. Find them in Overture Maps, which is anonymously readable with per-row licenses: a Jacksonville-sized bounding box returned **78,652 places, 10,656 with a phone and no website, and 8,144 of those open at confidence 0.5 or better** in under five seconds ([Overture release 2026-09-23.1 on S3](https://overturemaps-us-west-2.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=release/2026-09-23.1/theme=places/type=place/)); Google is a per-row verifier keyed by `place_id`, never a source. The app menu needs no partner contract to launch: Stripe Connect Standard, Square, Mailchimp, Shopify import, QuickBooks, Xero, Calendly, Shippo and Lightspeed X-Series expose self-serve OAuth, ShipStation, WooCommerce and Toast standard access are paste-a-key, and the hard gates (Google Business Profile, Meta, DoorDash Drive, Toast partner, OpenTable) are applications filed once and shipped when they clear ([Nango providers.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/packages/providers/providers.yaml)). The competitor is not Wix; it is the GoHighLevel- or Duda-powered local agency selling "$0 down, $149 a month" to the same phone-book businesses, and Thryv, whose **100,000 SaaS clients paid $373 a month on average in Q4 2025 ($356 for full-year 2025)** and whose Trustpilot profile is 73% one-star ([Thryv 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1556739/000155673926000013/thry-20251231.htm); [B12 review roundup](https://orchestra.b12.io/reviews/thryv/)), which hands the founder a positioning for free: month-to-month, the owner owns the domain, data and Stripe account, integrations wired for them. The CatholicJoe teardown shows a reusable server core and sixteen test suites, and the first platform release, now scoped to include hardening, a block-based frontend and the integrations framework, is **65–91 engineering days**, up from the teardown's 44–60 ([engine-architecture.md](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Cloudflare remains the right host, from a **$5-a-month plan minimum** that stays near $5–10 a month through 50 sites and $25–50 a month at 200, plus client domains passed through at cost (~$10 a year each); the naming shortlist (Statio, Shopstead, Pundak) still needs the domain script run from an unproxied machine; and every vendor price here comes from open-source docs repositories, SEC filings or third-party trackers because the sandbox blocked the vendors' own sites.

## Remote-first done-for-you is the model a solo founder can carry

The founder's constraint is time, and the operating model has to respect that before anything else. The three directions the earlier research weighed, productized service, managed platform and self-serve SaaS, are stages rather than peers, and the public record still says service first, then managed platform, and never self-serve SaaS first: Volusion's live stores fell roughly **74%** after its 2020 bankruptcy ([kensium](https://www.kensium.com/blog/ecommerce-options-for-volusion-customers-post-bankruptcy)), Tictail and Selz were acquired and closed ([ecommercebytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2018/11/22/tictail-to-close-after-shopify-acquires-the-marketplace/); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/companies/latest-news-on-Selz--26509)), while the entrant that worked was vertical and sales-led, Owner.com, at **$249–$499 a month, month-to-month, with more than $100M ARR and a $2.3B valuation** ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/owner-raises-240-million-at-2-3-billion-valuation-as-arr-tops-100-million/); [getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees)). What the founder's vision changes is the delivery mechanism: remote-first is a design requirement, and a drive to a local shop is a brand tactic that slots into the same flow rather than something the business depends on. That matches the published onboarding playbook, intake form, proposal, build, finalise, test, launch, maintain ([Wix Studio](https://wix.com/studio/blog/optimize-your-web-design-process); [CoordinateHQ](https://www.coordinatehq.com/solutions-articles/mastering-the-web-design-client-onboarding-process-a-comprehensive-guide)), and the evidence that a 4–5 page site takes "a few hours to one weekend if your photos and content are ready" ([PhotoBiz](https://blog.photobiz.com/website-builder/how-fast-can-you-really-build-a-website)), which makes asset collection, not design, the critical path, and asset collection works over a phone and an upload link as well as at a counter. The engine document turns the playbook into a seven-step flow in which only the review call and content collection benefit from presence, so a business a thousand miles away gets the identical product ([engine-architecture.md §9](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)).

Why done-for-you rather than a cheaper self-serve builder is answered by who the hold-outs are. Clutch's August 2025 survey found **83% of US small businesses have a website; of the 17% that do not, only 16% cite cost**, while 34% say a site is "not relevant to my industry" and 35% say they are "too small to need one" ([Clutch via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250821848152/en/Clutch-Report-No-Code-Tools-Fuel-Website-Growth-Yet-17-of-Small-Businesses-are-Still-Offline)); one-third of the smallest businesses have no site ([Small Business Majority](https://smallbusinessmajority.org/node/389880)); and **51% of US small businesses are owned by people over 55**, with 30–37% of owners in their fifties through seventies calling "getting online" a top challenge ([Gene Marks](https://www.genemarks.com/columns/most-small-business-owners-are-middle-aged-not-cool-kids-the-media-loves); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/work/small-business/starting-microbusiness/)). These owners are effort-blocked, not price-blocked, so a $16-a-month AI builder they must operate themselves does not reach them, and the Hostinger compilation that **73% of small-business owners plan to use AI for web design or content by end-2026** (unverified, vendor compilation) ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/ai-website-builder)) describes the other 83%. What AI builders do not do is the owner's work: photos, menu, hours, Google Business Profile, domain and DNS, Stripe, ShipStation, Mailchimp, and the phone call when something breaks. That bundle is what the subscription sells, and the vision's long-term conversational builder should be a retention feature for subscribers rather than an acquisition product that competes with Wix AI and Squarespace Blueprint on their own turf ([viability note Q6](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/viability_solo_founder.md)).

The pricing ladder spans the vision's full range, from a $500 presence site to $40k–$100k custom software, but each rung has a different job. The $500 tier has no durable margin against AI builders and attracts the wrong customer; Designjoy's founder reports that **"$500 clients proved demanding and unreliable"** while $5,000 clients valued efficiency ([onepage-research summary](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/youtube.com/yt-dXKzST0FE-A)). It survives only as a priced-to-convert entry, "$500 down then $99 a month" or "$0 down, $149 a month, twelve months", whose deliverable is explicitly the done-for-you work. The managed tiers are where the family income lives, priced inside the established market band: "$0 down, $99–$199 a month, 12-month minimum" is a widely advertised website-as-a-service offer ([redefineweb](https://redefineweb.com/services/pay-monthly-websites-design-smb/); [Digital Main Street](https://digitalmainstreet.ca/product/simple-solution-e-commerce/)), care plans run $50–$500 a month ([apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost)), GoHighLevel agencies resell at $97–$297 ([webdew](https://www.webdew.com/blog/gohighlevel-saas-mode)), and a Duda customer "moved from giving most websites away for free to consistently charging $99 per month" ([casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/duda/case-study/broadly-boosts-mrr-29-with-duda)). Above that, Thryv's **$373 Q4 2025 average and 69% of SaaS revenue from clients paying over $400 a month** ([MarketChameleon](https://marketchameleon.com/articles/b/2026/2/27/thryv-saas-growth-ai-platform-2025-results)) show what phone-book businesses pay when a bundle is sold to them, while the Mewayz report's **median of $124 a month on all software for 1–10 employee businesses** (vendor-published, sample unverified) ([Mewayz](https://mewayz.com/en/blog/smb-software-spend-report-2026-how-much-small-businesses-actually-pay)) is what they pay when they self-serve; the founder's price sits between: sold, not self-served, below the reseller tier. The custom tier is anchored by the family store and by market rates of **$15,000–$50,000+ for e-commerce and integration builds** ([TechLion relaying Clutch](https://www.techlion.dev/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-small-business)) and $8,000–$50,000 for boutique agencies ([ParallelHQ](https://parallelhq.com/blog/website-design-prices)).

The margin structure makes every rung work. Cloudflare Workers Paid is **$5 a month per account, not per site** ([costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/cloud-infrastructure/cloudflare-pages-workers/)) against $5–$17 per site on Duda, Sitejet or Brizy ([sitejet.io](https://www.sitejet.io/en/pricing); [joinsecret](https://www.joinsecret.com/duda/pricing)), so cost of goods per site is under $10 a month and contribution margin before labor exceeds 90% (computed from those figures). Productized services run **60–90% gross margin against about 40% for custom work** ([Shiprocket](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/productised-services/)), and vertical-focused agencies report **14.3% net margin versus 8.6% for generalists and 2.5x the referrals** ([Hinge via HubSpot](https://hingemarketing.com/about-hinge/news-events/news/article/hubspot-publishes-hinge-article-practices-that-gain-client-referrals)), which is the quantitative case for the two-vertical discipline in the next section. Keep revenue in software and service subscriptions and leave payments with Stripe on the client's own account: Wix earns **80% gross margin on Creative Subscriptions but 33% on Business Solutions** ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Wix+Reports+Second+Quarter+2026+Results/26859973.html)) and Shopify's blended margin was 47.7% in Q2 2026 because payments revenue grew faster than subscriptions ([Shopify 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001594805/000159480526000047/shop-20260630.htm)). Stripe Connect Standard is free and leaves the client as merchant of record, against $2 per active account plus 0.25% and $0.25 per payout for Express or Custom ([paymentcloudinc](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/stripe-connect/)).

Two churn regimes exist and the model must sit in the low one. "Website as plumbing", where the provider holds hosting, domain, DNS and the integrations, churns only when the business dies: one developer reported **45 clients over five years with only two subscriptions discontinued** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/two-sales-tips-for-freelance-web-devs-dd204a2e56)); "care plan on a site the client could move" churns **20–35% a year** ([kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/retain-web-clients-reduce-churn-agency-2026)). Integrations the owner connected through OAuth inside the back-office, with the engine holding orders, subscribers and the Google Business Profile sync, are the plumbing that makes leaving painful; a site that merely links to the owner's own Square page has no lock-in, which is why the engine stores every integration as a connection record the tenant owns ([engine-architecture.md §7.2](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Maintenance is the other number to design for: one operator with 30 WordPress sites reports **20–30 minutes per site per month** ([ishouldbeyourwpguy](https://ishouldbeyourwpguy.com/how-much-do-you-need-to-spend-to-maintain-your-website/)); at 0.5–1 hour per site including edits, 70 sites is 35–70 hours a month before any selling, which only works if onboarding is under 8 founder-hours per client and demos and configs are generated rather than hand-built ([viability note Q9](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/viability_solo_founder.md)).

## Trades, auto repair and salons lead the ranked inventory, and Overture finds them

Thirty-five business types were inventoried and scored on six weighted criteria: pool size 15%, share without a website 25%, willingness and ability to pay 20%, decision-maker reachability 15%, engine fit 15% and support burden 10%, each scored 1–5 from the evidence and, where no evidence exists, from a labelled estimate ([business_types_ranking.md](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/business_types_ranking.md)). The scores are judgments, not measurements, but the top five are robust to the weights because they lead on both no-site share and engine fit, and three of them lead on pay. The common thread is a large pool of single-location, owner-run businesses where the owner answers the shop phone and the presence-and-services preset is the whole product. The only type-level no-website surveys that exist all point at home and property services: **over 40% of home-service businesses have no website** (Housecall Pro) ([PM Magazine](https://pmmag.com/articles/102211-housecall-pro-helps-home-service-companies-build-free-professional-websites)), **more than half of small businesses** in Jobber's framing ([HVAC Insider](https://hvacinsider.com/jobber-empowers-home-service-pros-to-attract-more-customers-and-grow-smarter-with-new-digital-marketing-tools/)), and **124,203 US house-cleaning companies operating across only 53,227 websites**, an implied 57% without ([Home Service Base](https://homeservicebase.com/reports/house-cleaning-us)). The pools are large: **692,777 landscaping businesses** ([IBISWorld](https://img3.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/landscaping-services/1497)), **111,200 plumbing and HVAC establishments** ([Vanta Insights](https://vantainsights.com/reports/238220-plumbing-heating-air-conditioning)), **128,187 residential remodelers** at a record high ([Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2025/07/residential-remodelers-outnumber-single-family-builders-in-the-u-s/)), and **176,403 auto repair establishments, three in four single-location owner-operators** ([Orbital](https://www.withorbital.com/data/how-many-auto-repair-shops-in-the-us/)). These owners already pay Housecall Pro ($59–$299) or Jobber ($29–$399 on annual billing, $49–$499 month-to-month) monthly for leads and scheduling ([ContractorToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/reports/contractor-software-pricing-2026/)), which means they understand paying monthly for a front door.

Restaurants rank eighth despite the largest pool and the founder's named use case because the Technomic data shows a shrinking sector and the TouchBistro data shows the gap is direct ordering, not any website. A presence site with a structured menu and a link to the Square, Toast or DoorDash Storefront ordering the restaurant already has is sellable at $99–$149 and competes with free; order routing is not something to build first, which is why the food preset's depth (modifiers, pickup slots, Square and Clover order sync) is phase 9 of the engine plan, after a paying restaurant exists ([engine-architecture.md §12](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Restaurants and every other local type remain in scope; the ranking decides the order of outbound effort, not who may buy. The secondary segment, businesses with a site that want to switch or add integrations, is chosen by integration pull rather than size: physical-goods retailers and makers for whom the CatholicJoe stack is literally the product; restaurants paying Owner.com or ChowNow $119–$499 a month who want to own the menu and the email list; home-service firms on the free Housecall Pro and Jobber builders who are "often unhappy with their site's appearance, functionality and/or cost" ([PM Magazine](https://pmmag.com/articles/102211-housecall-pro-helps-home-service-companies-build-free-professional-websites)); professional practices where **only 26% of dental practices offer online scheduling while 82% of patients book from a phone** ([Clerri](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-practice-scheduling-statistics/)), best treated as $2k–$10k projects because every integration adds HIPAA and support burden; and parts and hardware retailers with legacy inventory, the custom tier. Shopify took **70% of e-commerce platform migrations in 2024** and integrations drive about 30% of mid-sized retailers' moves (vendor figures, unverified) ([Cart2Cart](https://www.shopping-cart-migration.com/must-know-tips/cart2cart-migration-facts-statistics)), which is the pull the "switch to us" tiles answer.

Each top type launches on five to seven pages and one type-specific integration the owner already uses (Housecall Pro or Jobber for trades, Square or Toast for food, Booksy or Square Appointments for salons), and the founder's largest content cost is the same everywhere: photos and a services or price list the owner rarely has, whether that is a plumber's license numbers and 10–20 job photos, a landscaper's before-and-after shots, or a cafe's chalkboard menu ([business_types_ranking.md Q4](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/business_types_ranking.md)). These checklists are now preset data: `tooling/scaffold` substitutes intake values into placeholder block props (name into the hero, phone into the call-to-action, hours into the hours block, place id into the reviews block), which is what makes a demo cost minutes rather than hours ([engine-architecture.md §6](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)).

### Overture is the prospect source; Google verifies; the states time the signal

The discovery question the founder asked, a reliable way to find businesses with an address and a phone but no website, has a measured answer. Overture Maps release 2026-09-23.1 is **16 zstd Parquet files, 11.0 GB and 81,455,423 places**, anonymously readable from `s3://overturemaps-us-west-2`, with `websites`, `socials`, `emails`, `phones`, `addresses`, `confidence`, `operating_status` and a per-row `sources[].license` ([Overture S3 listing](https://overturemaps-us-west-2.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=release/2026-09-23.1/theme=places/type=place/); [OvertureMaps/data README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OvertureMaps/data/main/README.md)). In a 1-in-16 row-group sample of **1,131,545 US rows, 87.7% carry a phone, 79.3% a website, 13.8% a phone and no website, and 10.3% pass the phone, no website, open-or-unknown and confidence ≥ 0.5 filter** (6.7% at confidence ≥ 0.75) (measured in-session; same source). Overture's 79.3% website share lines up with the 73–83% survey range, a useful check that the flag is not wildly over-counting ([Review42](https://resources.review42.com/what-percentage-of-small-businesses-have-a-website/)). The two metro measurements set expectations: a Chattanooga-sized bounding box returned **27,381 places, 3,347 with a phone and no website, 2,665 open at confidence ≥ 0.5 and 1,848 at ≥ 0.75**; the Jacksonville box returned **78,652, 10,656, 8,144 and 4,983**, each query in 3.7–4.6 seconds with row-group pruning on the `bbox` struct (same source). The top categories among flagged Jacksonville rows were real-estate agents (299), churches (285), beauty salons (252), hair salons (227), automotive repair (223), barbers (173), contractors (160), restaurants (144) and convenience stores (125), which is the ranked inventory reflected back from the data, plus the churches and agents the predicate prunes.

The DuckDB predicate is the whole pipeline in one statement: read the release's Parquet files, filter `bbox.xmin`, `bbox.xmax`, `bbox.ymin` and `bbox.ymax` to the metro rectangle, require `addresses[1].country = 'US'`, `len(phones) > 0`, `websites IS NULL OR len(websites) = 0`, `operating_status IS NULL OR operating_status = 'open'`, `confidence >= 0.5`, and exclude `taxonomy.primary` values that are not customers (places of worship, real-estate agents, historic sites, government offices, schools, cemeteries), then `COPY` the selected columns, including `sources[1].dataset`, `sources[1].license`, `sources[1].record_id` and `sources[1].update_time`, to a CSV ([no_website_discovery_pipeline.md Q9](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/no_website_discovery_pipeline.md)). The predicates were validated with pyarrow against the same files because the sandbox could not install DuckDB's `httpfs` and `spatial` extensions, which Overture's own guide requires ([OvertureMaps/docs duckdb.mdx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OvertureMaps/docs/main/docs/getting-data/duckdb.mdx)), so run the statement once on a laptop before automating it. After category pruning a mid-sized metro yields roughly 2,000–2,400 actionable rows (2,364 measured for Chattanooga, Overture release 2026-09-23.1, with the note's ten-category exclusion list; a heavier prune that also drops health, government and park categories still leaves about 2,100) and a Jacksonville-sized metro about 8,000 before pruning (estimate from the measurements); a nationwide table would be about 1.9M rows at confidence ≥ 0.5 (estimate, 16 times the sampled count), which fits D1 but far exceeds what one person can work, so load metros on demand. The load path that respects Cloudflare's limits is DuckDB on a laptop or a monthly GitHub Actions job, CSV to R2, then a Workflow that upserts in 50-row batches, because cron handlers cap at 15 minutes and 128 MB and cannot run DuckDB, and D1 allows 100 bound parameters per statement ([Workers limits](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/production/src/content/docs/workers/platform/limits.mdx); [D1 import/export](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/production/src/content/docs/d1/best-practices/import-export-data.mdx)).

The licensing conclusion is clean and machine-checkable. Every US row in the sample carried **CDLA-Permissive-2.0 (Meta, BrightQuery, Microsoft, DAC), Apache-2.0 (Foursquare) or CC0-1.0 (AllThePlaces), and no ODbL rows appeared** in the places theme (measured; [Overture S3 listing](https://overturemaps-us-west-2.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=release/2026-09-23.1/theme=places/type=place/); [Overture defs.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OvertureMaps/schema/main/schema/defs.yaml)). All three permit storage and commercial use in a private CRM; the Apache rows carry Foursquare's NOTICE-preservation duty, satisfied by keeping the NOTICE text in the repo and linking it from an "About data" page ([FSQ NOTICE.txt](https://fsq-os-places-us-east-1.s3.amazonaws.com/NOTICE.txt)). Foursquare's own S3 objects were not anonymously readable this session (every candidate path returned 404 and the Hugging Face mirror is reported gated), so Overture, which republishes the Foursquare rows, is the primary bulk source ([FSQ bucket listing](https://fsq-os-places-us-east-1.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&delimiter=/)). OpenStreetMap's ODbL share-alike bites only on public use, so an internal CRM has no sharing duty, but OSM-derived rows should be tagged and excluded from anything later published ([OSMF Licence FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ)). Google stays out of the database except `place_id`: the Maps Platform terms forbid bulk download, copying business names and addresses, and use in a listings or directory service ([Google Maps Platform Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)), and the Business Profile APIs may be used only for listings you own or are authorized to manage ([Business Profile API policies](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/policies)). Google's role is therefore a per-row verify: Text Search (New) to resolve a `place_id`, then Place Details with `websiteUri`, billed on the Enterprise SKU at **$20 per 1,000 with 1,000 free calls a month** ([Google pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)), which covers about 30 prospects a day at zero cost and a 2,000-row backlog for about $20. Data Axle through a library card is a research tool whose license prohibits direct-marketing use ([Data Axle terms](https://platform.data-axle.com/terms)), and the Google Maps "no website" scrapers that exist as a product category ([Apify](https://apify.com/leafy-dev-jr/no-website-business-leads-scraper-google-maps.md)) violate §3.2.3 and risk the Cloud account the client sites depend on.

Two refinements raise precision. First, **52–69% of flagged rows carry a Facebook URL in `socials`** (Chattanooga 2,312 of 3,347; Jacksonville 5,495 of 10,656; US sample 102,534 of 156,228) (measured; same source). Those Facebook-only businesses already believe in an online presence, their page supplies name, photos and hours for a demo (ask before reusing photos), and they should rank above rows with no presence at all, which skew to churches and agents. Second, state registries are a timing signal: Florida publishes free daily new-filing files over a public SFTP account ([Sunbiz daily data](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/other-services/data-downloads/daily-data/)), Georgia sells the full corporations file for **$1,000 one-time or $100 setup plus $500 a month** ([Georgia bulk data](https://georgia.gov/bulk-corporations-data)), and Texas sells a **$20-per-week new-filings CSV** ([Texas SOS](https://direct.sos.state.tx.us/help/help-corp.asp?pg=bulk)); none carry a phone or category, so a new LLC that then appears in Overture with a phone and no website, joined on normalized name plus postcode, is the highest-intent prospect. The verification ladder runs cheapest first: libphonenumber-js normalization ([libphonenumber README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/libphonenumber/master/README.md)), a webmail check on `emails`, DNS probes of name-derived domains through Cloudflare's DNS-JSON endpoint ([Cloudflare dns-json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/production/src/content/docs/1.1.1.1/encryption/dns-over-https/make-api-requests/dns-json.mdx)), one Brave Search query at about $5 per 1,000 ([Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-search-apis/brave-search-api/)), then the Google call, then a human look at the Facebook page and the Google listing. No published precision figure exists for "no website" flags; expect 20–40% of raw flags to be non-prospects (estimate, from the Facebook-only share, Overture's provider union and the 2–4% measured closure rate) and measure the first 200 rows to tune the thresholds ([no_website_discovery_pipeline.md Q7](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/no_website_discovery_pipeline.md)). The outreach rules are those of the dashboard section below: CAN-SPAM email to published business addresses, founder-dialled calls to business lines, no SMS without consent, demos built from nominative use of the name only.

The ship order follows gating and value, and nothing in the first two waves requires a marketplace listing or a partner program; the only vendor steps are light ones: QuickBooks withholds production keys until Intuit has reviewed its 30–40 minute assessment questionnaire, which applies to unlisted apps as well as listed ones, Shippo issues platform OAuth credentials on request (company name, callback URL and contact email sent to its sales team), and Stripe's Connect platform activation is a one-time platform review filed in wave 1 (new work, since CatholicJoe runs on its own keys today). Wave 0 goes on every site: Cloudflare Web Analytics, GA4 and the Meta Pixel through Zaraz, the Google reviews block with its mandatory attribution, and transactional email through Resend, whose idempotency key lets the engine retry an ambiguous send rather than hold it forever as the Cloudflare binding forces ([engine-architecture.md §4.6](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Wave 1 builds on the vendors already in production, not on their platform auth modes: CatholicJoe talks to Stripe with its own secret key and webhook secret, Mailchimp is a hosted signup form of three public identifiers, and only the ShipStation V2 key is the mechanism the engine keeps as-is. Stripe Connect Account Links and Mailchimp OAuth are new work in this wave; the Stripe and ShipStation webhook code is lifted unchanged ([engine-architecture.md §7.2, §12](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Wave 2 is the self-serve, highest-value set: Square OAuth, where one grant covers menu, orders, inventory, bookings and webhooks for food, retail and salons alike; Shippo OAuth as the default shipping tile because it is the only shipping connector that satisfies "the owner never sees an API key" and also serves merchants without a shipping account; Calendly; the Shopify, WooCommerce and CSV import tiles; QuickBooks and Xero, whose uncertified app connects instantly and is capped at 25 connections until certified ([Nango Xero guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/docs/api-integrations/xero/how-to-register-your-own-xero-api-oauth-app.mdx)). Wave 3 is "apply now, ship when approved": Google Business Profile, whose application requires a verified profile at least 60 days old that already lists a website, so the founder's own studio profile is the gating asset and should be created the day the brand is chosen; Clover; Meta. Deferred, with a paste-credentials or link fallback, are PayPal partner referrals, Toast, DoorDash Drive, OpenTable, Resy, Twilio SMS and any marketplace listing; every gated program wants a demo or a business-development contact and most want a live merchant, so the family store and the first local restaurant are the credible launch plan those reviews ask for, and partner applications are sequenced after the first two live sites ([integrations_fulfillment_ordering_inventory.md Q5](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/integrations_fulfillment_ordering_inventory.md); [engine-architecture.md §7.8](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). The "integrates with DoorDash" promise in the vision is therefore achievable in three senses with very different effort: a button on day one, Drive white-label delivery once production access is granted, and Marketplace menu sync only through an aggregator.

Build versus Nango is a question about the OAuth broker only, because the business logic, the webhooks and the state machines stay in the Worker regardless. Nango is licensed under the Elastic License 2.0, which forbids offering the software "to third parties as a hosted or managed service" but otherwise grants a royalty-free license to use, copy and "prepare derivative works" ([Nango LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/LICENSE)); an owner's back-office is the founder's own product, not a resale of Nango, so both using it and porting from it are permitted. Its `providers.yaml` lists **1,038 providers** and encodes every quirk the first ten integrations would otherwise cost days to discover: Shopify's shop-subdomain authorize URL and, since December 2025, hourly-rotating offline tokens; Clover's regional hosts; QuickBooks' `realmId`; Xero's tenant lookup; Mailchimp's data-center capture; Toast's two-step login; Square's `session=false` ([Nango providers.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/packages/providers/providers.yaml); [Nango Shopify](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/docs/api-integrations/shopify.mdx)). Nango Cloud's 2026 prices are secondary and conflicting, a free tier of about 10 connections then roughly $50 a month (unverified) ([toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/nango/pricing)), and self-hosting is split: Enterprise-only for the vendor-supported BYOC and Self-Managed paths (fixed annual license, all Cloud features), while a free docker-compose self-host of the public `nangohq/nango-server:hosted` image remains available for Auth and Proxy only, with no syncs, webhooks, tool calls or SSO and no vendor support ([Nango self-hosting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/docs/guides/platform/self-hosting/self-hosting.mdx)). Since the engine uses Nango purely as an OAuth broker and routes webhooks straight to the Worker, the free self-host is a viable third option alongside Nango Cloud and the in-house port. The unified-API vendors are priced for funded SaaS and normalize away the provider depth the engine needs: Merge is **$650 a month after three linked accounts** ([Knit on Merge pricing](https://lb.getknit.dev/blog/understanding-merge-dev-pricing-finding-the-right-unified-api-for-your-integration-needs.md)), Apideck **EUR 599 a month** ([Knit on Apideck](https://getknit.dev/blog/a-detailed-evaluation-of-apideck-as-a-unified-api-platform)), and Paragon publishes no prices. The recommendation, and the engine's design, is an `OAuthBroker` interface with two implementations: in-house for the first three providers regardless, because Stripe uses Account Links rather than OAuth, Square is a plain OAuth2 app and Mailchimp is a no-scope OAuth2 app, roughly 300–500 lines in a Worker plus a `connections` table for the generic OAuth2 flow across ten providers (the note's estimate; the first three are a subset of that, and Stripe's Account Links path is not OAuth at all); and Nango Cloud for the long tail (QuickBooks, Xero, Shopify, BigCommerce, Lightspeed, Clover, Calendly, Klaviyo, Constant Contact, Wave), replaceable later by an in-house port of `providers.yaml` ([engine-architecture.md §7.3](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Payment webhooks go straight to the Worker in either case, because Nango forwards webhooks with a **20-second timeout and two retries** ([Nango limits](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NangoHQ/nango/master/docs/guides/platform/limits.mdx)), weaker than Stripe's own schedule, and because the CatholicJoe pattern of persisting the event before any work is the one every adapter should inherit. Per-tenant secrets live as Worker secrets on the tenant's own Worker, 128 variables at 5 KB each on Paid ([Workers limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workers/platform/limits.mdx)), with `connections.secret_ref` naming the variable and never holding the value.

## Who the competition is depends on which customer is in front of you

The founder's product meets a different rival at each rung of the ladder, and the useful competitor analysis is per customer archetype rather than per category. Five layers exist. DIY builders (Wix $17–$159, Squarespace $16–$99 annual, Shopify Basic $39, Square Online free) ([BlackBeltCommerce](https://www.blackbeltcommerce.com/compare-shopify-vs-wix-vs-squarespace-find-your-best-fit/)) win on price and brand but leave the owner to do the work; AI site builders (Durable $15–$99, B12 from $42, Hostinger $2.99 renewing at $10.99, 10Web $10, Framer $10–$30) ([websitesetup](https://websitesetup.org/ai-website-builders/); [Web3AIBlog](https://www.web3aiblog.com/blog/best-ai-website-builders-2026)) have made a generic brochure site nearly free in effort, and Durable has generated more than 6 million sites while describing revenue only as "material" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2640709)), evidence that AI-generated presence sites alone are a volume game with weak monetization; AI app builders (Lovable at **$400M ARR in February 2026**, Base44 bought by Wix for $80M at six months old) ([Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/comparisons/base44-vs-lovable)) are the founder's future self-serve substrate, not today's rival for a plumber; agency platforms (Duda $19–$149, GoHighLevel $97–$497, Sitejet, Brizy, Vendasta) ([Capterra Duda](https://www.capterra.com/p/134280/DudaMobile/pricing); [GHL Experts](https://ghlexperts.com/gohighlevel-plans-pricing)) are cost comparables that prove agencies pay $150–$500 a month for a multi-tenant engine the founder already owns on Cloudflare; and local marketing resellers (Thryv, Hibu, LocalIQ, Townsquare Interactive, Scorpion) sell bundled site, listings, ads and CRM under contract. Thryv is the largest listed done-for-you reseller: **$461.0M of SaaS revenue in 2025, 100,000 clients, $373 Q4 2025 SaaS monthly ARPU (full-year 2025 average $356), 94% net revenue retention and a 16% SaaS EBITDA margin**, while its legacy marketing-services line fell 32.6% toward a planned 2028 exit ([Thryv 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001556739/000155673926000013/thry-20251231.htm); [Thryv Q4 2025 press release, 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1556739/000155673926000012/exhibit991-pressreleaseq42.htm); [MarketChameleon](https://marketchameleon.com/articles/b/2026/2/27/thryv-saas-growth-ai-platform-2025-results)). Thryv's own numbers size the outflow: the 32.6% marketing-services decline is roughly $157M a year (from about $481M to $324.0M), and 94% net revenue retention on its established SaaS cohort implies about 6% net attrition on a $461M base, roughly $28M, so on the order of $150–$190M a year of small-business spend is leaving Thryv's legacy reseller model, with about $20M of that converting into Thryv's own SaaS rather than leaving the company, and the complaint pattern is consistent: 73% one-star reviews with reports of a $10,000 cancellation fee and features sold that did not exist (hostile aggregation by competitor B12; treat as such) ([B12 Orchestra](https://orchestra.b12.io/reviews/thryv/); [Trustpilot Thryv](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/thryv.com)), Townsquare's $300–$500 a month with 30–90 day cancellation notice and "tasks half done" ([Trustpilot Townsquare](https://se.trustpilot.com/review/townsquareinteractive.com)), and LocalIQ users reporting unreturned calls ([G2 LocalIQ](https://g2.com/products/localiq/pricing)). That pattern writes the founder's positioning: month-to-month, owner owns domain, content and Stripe account, written scope, a named human, on the pricing page rather than only in the pitch.

The no-website service business is the strongest lane and the archetype where the competitor is a free-but-basic site inside Jobber or Housecall Pro, or a reseller's phone call. Both vertical platforms now bundle website builders wired to their booking and quote tools ([PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/9644-housecall-pro-offers-website-buider); [HVAC Insider](https://hvacinsider.com/jobber-empowers-home-service-pros-to-attract-more-customers-and-grow-smarter-with-new-digital-marketing-tools/)); ServiceTitan runs $245–$500 per technician with $5,000–$50,000 implementations and is not a small-shop product ([Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/housecall-pro-vs-servicetitan/)). The founder should not fight the job software; the site should post its quote and booking forms into it. The win is a better front door, month-to-month at $99–$199, with the reseller horror stories as the contrast. The no-website restaurant faces free (Square Online, DoorDash Storefront, a Facebook page) and Owner.com's sales reps at $249 plus 5% or $499 flat ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees)), with Popmenu at $179–$499, ChowNow at $229–$449 a month plus a $119–$499 setup fee, BentoBox at $49 plus $0.99 an order and Slice at 5% of sales in between ([getsauce BentoBox](https://www.getsauce.com/post/bentobox-pricing-fees); [ChowNow pricing](https://get.chownow.com/pricing/); [Slice pricing](https://slice.com/pricing)). Do not compete on ordering: sell a $99–$149 menu-and-brand site that links to whatever ordering the restaurant has and owns the email list, and expect the owner to compare against free, so the pitch is photos, Google ranking and direct-order links that cut the 15–30% marketplace commission. The retailer leaving Shopify faces Shopify's own inertia plus Wix and Squarespace; merchant complaints center on removed phone support, fee increases and stacked buy-now-pay-later fees ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-phone-support-has-been-removed/227138?page=2); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/shopify-complaints-mount-from-online-retailers-and-consumers-110419.html)), and a February 2026 crawl found 1.32 million domains that had churned off Shopify ([TechnologyChecker](https://www.technologychecker.io/blog/shopify-analytics-trends-insights)). Win with a migration (custom-app product import, Stripe plus Shippo plus Mailchimp pre-wired), 0% take rate and a human on the phone, priced at or below Shopify Basic plus its $30–$80 app stack ([websitebuilderexpert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/ecommerce-website-builders/shopify-pricing/)). The auto parts or hardware store wanting inventory online faces "do nothing" or a Square for Retail ($0–$149) or Lightspeed ($89–$289) template that cannot do fitment or sync a legacy system ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-pos-systems); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/reviews/small-business/lightspeed-retail-pos)), and a local dev-shop quote of $8,000–$50,000 and up ([ParallelHQ](https://parallelhq.com/blog/website-design-prices)); this is the custom tier, where the engine supplies storefront, cart, Stripe and shipping and the custom spend goes into the inventory sync and the licensed vehicle data. The salon or barber faces GlossGenius at $24, Vagaro at $30 per calendar and Booksy's marketplace ([Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/compare/glossgenius-vs-vagaro/)); hair salons have the lowest website adoption of any type, and the sale is a gallery, a price list and a booking link, at the entry tier.

Across all five archetypes the founder is never the cheapest option, and the only defensible claim is "it is done, it is wired to your tools, you own it, you can leave." No competitor found explicitly combines done-for-you build, an owner-facing OAuth integration menu, month-to-month pricing and a custom-software tier; that absence is a positioning opportunity, and it could also mean the bundle is hard to sell, which is unproven either way ([competitors.md Q6](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/competitors.md)). The durability risk is that Wix (Base44), GoDaddy (Airo) and Hostinger will make AI-generated sites free, so the moat must be the integration layer, the relationship and the custom tier, never the site generator. Against vertical SaaS the differentiator is neutrality: the founder's front door works with whichever Jobber, Square or Toast the owner already uses and survives a switch between them. The weakest point of the thesis is restaurants, where ordering is the product; the strongest are home services, salons and independent retail and makers, which matches the ranking.

## The business makes sense, and the solo constraint is hours, not demand

The viability verdict is yes with a shape, and the numbers are specific enough to plan on. Take the **2025 median household income of $87,460** as the target ([Census via AP/Fox 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/americans-household-income-higher-data)) and assume a 1.25x gross-up for roughly 14% self-employment tax and about $9,000 a year of family health insurance (assumption; replace with the founder's own figures), which gives about **$109,300 of gross profit a year or $9,100 a month**; a $100,000 take-home needs about $10,400 a month. Cost of goods per site is under $10 a month and is ignored, which understates client counts by 2–4% at $149 and above ([viability note Q1](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/viability_solo_founder.md)). Setup fees of $500–$1,500 and the custom builds' cash are deliberately excluded from the recurring tables; they are year-one working capital, not income the family can plan on.

Time to those numbers is modelled rather than observed, because no solo operator's dated milestones were retrievable: at 15% annual churn and $149 ARPU, three net-new sites a month reaches about $2,600 MRR at month 6 and $5,000 at month 12; four a month reaches $5,100 at month 9 and $6,700 at month 12; five a month at $179 reaches $10,000 at month 12. The plausible band is therefore **$5k MRR in 9–12 months and $10k in 12–18**, and the custom-build line, one $40k build being $3,300 a month in equivalent, is what makes year one survivable ([viability note Q2](/home/user/consultingTemplate/research_notes/Platform%20segments%20integrations%20competition/viability_solo_founder.md)). Demand is not the constraint: **36.2 million US small businesses** with 1.1 million new establishments a year ([SBA Advocacy](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2025/10/28/advocacy-releases-2025-small-business-profiles-for-major-metropolitan-areas/)), **491,941–578,926 business applications a month** in 2026 ([Census BFS March 2026](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/business-formation-statistics-april8.html); [Census BFS July 2026](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/business-formation-statistics-jul9.html)), and 8,144 open, phone-listed, website-less businesses in the Jacksonville box alone before category pruning (a mid-sized metro like Chattanooga yields roughly 2,000–2,400 actionable rows after it) against a need for 60–100 of them. The flip side is closure: **34.7% of establishments born in 2013 were still operating in 2023** and five-year survival sits near 50% ([BLS TED](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/34-7-percent-of-business-establishments-born-in-2013-were-still-operating-in-2023.htm)), so a base weighted toward new formations loses 15–20% a year to closures regardless of service quality, while a base of established businesses churns well under 10% (inference from the survival curves). Mix both, and track churn by cohort so closure churn is not mistaken for product churn.

Why yes: the founder already owns the two things that cost competitors most, a production commerce stack that most $149 website-as-a-service sellers cannot match and a near-zero per-site cost of goods; the family store is a real $40k-class custom client and the case study for the retail variant; the hold-outs are effort-blocked rather than price-blocked, which is exactly what done-for-you solves; and managed subscriptions to established small businesses churn mostly on closure, so the base compounds. Why it can still fail: the model tops out around 100–150 sites per person even with automation (estimate), so "scale without the founder" requires the conversational editor or a first hire near 80 sites; year one is cash-poor, with 3–4 sites a month yielding only $5–7k MRR at month 12; and every documented failure mode is a time leak. Agencies "usually stall because of internal operational issues like weak niche focus, ignored SOPs, and founder dependency, not external market conditions," and the scrappiest structure "breaks somewhere between 8 and 15 clients" ([SaaS Hero](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/common-growth-agency-mistakes/)); scope creep ends in burnout ([Very Good Productized Guides](https://verygoodproductizedguides.substack.com/p/i-got-burnt-out-doing-custom-work)); maintenance requests under a retainer are "awkward to say no" to and compound across clients ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/why-growing-agencies-struggle-with-website-maintenance-demands/)); and hand-built demos are more than 90% waste against a cold-email baseline of **3 replies from 820 emails** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/800-cold-emails-later-heres-what-actually-moves-the-needle-and-what-s-a-complete-waste-of-time-1e67d7e295)). Operators who stay solo cap client count, force asynchronous one-at-a-time requests and raise prices rather than add staff; Designjoy reached $1.3–$1.7M a year with no employees by capping at 20–35 clients and raising prices nine times ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/ideas/design-agency/success-stories); [Indie Hackers AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-make-50k-m-running-my-solo-unlimited-design-service-ama-133d3bba9d)).

The twelve-month plan sells before it builds, and the engine plan is sequenced to match it: phases 0, 1, 2 (presence preset only) and the scaffold half of phase 4 first, with integrations limited to wave 0–1 tiles, before the founder dashboard, because the CRM can be a spreadsheet for the first ten accounts and the per-tenant engine cannot ([engine-architecture.md §12](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). The food preset is absent from year one on purpose and enters only when a restaurant pays for it. Five risks matter, each with a mitigation already in the plan. First, the solo capacity ceiling: config-only onboarding at under 8–10 founder-hours per site, asynchronous one-request-at-a-time support, hours measured monthly, and a part-time hire or the conversational editor at about 80 sites. Second, the year-one cash gap: the family-store build in month 1 and a second custom proposal by month 7, setup fees and annual prepay as working capital, and any current income kept until MRR passes $5k. Fifth, concentration and closure churn from one metro, new-formation prospects and two verticals: mix new-formation leads with established businesses using Overture's confidence and recency filters, expand to a second metro once the demo pipeline runs at 200 a month, and track churn by cohort. ## Statio, Shopstead and Pundak survive the naming gauntlet

Seventy-five candidates were checked (your thirty plus some forty-five more generated in the same mercantile-outpost spirit) against npm, GitHub, web evidence of a live business at the .com, pronunciation and spelling risk, hidden meanings, and the "pizza shop" and "VC room" tests. Two findings frame the result. First, roughly two-thirds of your thirty (20 of 30) and over half of the full table are dead on arrival, most of them because a live commerce, SaaS or other company already uses the name and the rest because the name fails on pronunciation, hidden meaning or already-taken npm and GitHub handles: Mercatus is a grocery e-commerce storefront platform ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/mercatus-technologies)), WayStation is an AI website-creation platform for agencies ([SimilarLabs](https://similarlabs.com/es/p/ai-website-service)), Dukkan "digitizes small local businesses" ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/dukkan)), Stoa raised $2.4M in July 2026 ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/06/stoa-secures-24m-for-cash-rewards-platform/)), Torg is a B2B food marketplace ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/torg_)), Provi is a $750M-valuation alcohol marketplace ([Provi](https://www.provi.com/about-us)), Sundry is an apparel brand ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/sundry-clothing)), and Commissary is dominated by the Defense Commissary Agency, failing your no-military rule outright ([DeCA](https://corp.commissaries.com/node/8376)); Vicus also brushes that rule because a vicus was the civilian settlement outside a Roman fort ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/vicus)). The npm package `statio` exists ([registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/statio)) but `statiohq` is free ([registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/statiohq)), the GitHub user `statio` exists ([github.com/statio](https://github.com/statio)) and the org `statiohq` is also taken by a dormant 2021 project that called itself Statio (a realtime service named Funk; [github.com/statiohq](https://github.com/statiohq)), so neither bare `statio` nor `statiohq` is available on GitHub; the software collisions are all dormant: a Python sliding-window statistics library ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/statio), v0.0.2), the near-empty npm package `statio` (created 2019, latest 1.0.24, last published May 2022, readme "# state") and that 2021 TypeScript project branded "Statio" under the GitHub org `statiohq` (Funk realtime service; four repos, no pushes since December 2021) ([github.com/statiohq/funk.js](https://github.com/statiohq/funk.js)). Its risks are a pronunciation split ("STAH-tee-oh" versus "STAY-shee-oh") and search-result confusability with Statista. The recommended package is statio.com if RDAP shows it unregistered or a registrar lists it for sale, otherwise statiohq.com as primary with statio.co and statio.app as redirects; GitHub org `getstatio` or `statio-commerce` (both returned zero GitHub login hits and are free on npm as of 2026-10-05), npm scope matching whichever org is chosen (`@getstatio/*` or `@statio-commerce/*`; `@statiohq/*` has no packages but scope reservation could not be checked without npm auth), @statiohq on social treated as likely contested given the prior project, and a trademark search for STATIO and formatives in classes 9, 35 and 42 before filing in 35 and 42. The language traps worth noting beyond the shortlist (dictionary-level readings; no native-speaker review was done) are Pinta (Spanish slang and an image editor), Piska (Slavic childish or vulgar term), Mazon (one letter from Amazon), Fiscus (the tax authority in Dutch and German), and Taberna (a tavern in Spanish and Portuguese, fine for pizza shops but odd for Etsy sellers).

## Cloudflare is right: one Worker and one D1 per client, from $5 a month

Cloudflare is the correct platform, and the reason is as much about direction of travel as about price. The Pages documentation now carries the banner "Workers supports most Pages use cases and offers a broader feature set. It is Cloudflare's primary platform for building applications. Start new projects with Workers" ([Cloudflare Pages docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/)); the migration guide's compatibility matrix lists Cron Triggers, Email Workers, Queue consumers, Gradual Deployments and Logpush as Workers-only, and marks Durable Objects as unsupported on Pages except via a workaround (binding to a separate Worker that hosts the Durable Object) ([Migrate from Pages](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/migration-guides/migrate-from-pages/)); and the Astro adapter docs state, under "Removed: Cloudflare Pages support", that "The Astro Cloudflare adapter no longer supports deployment on Cloudflare Pages. For the best experience and feature support, you should migrate to Cloudflare Workers," with Astro 6 moving runtime access to `import { env } from 'cloudflare:workers'` ([Astro Cloudflare adapter docs](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare/)). CatholicJoe already runs Astro 7.3.4 with `@astrojs/cloudflare` 14.3.3, static assets and `wrangler deploy` ([package.json](/home/user/catholicjoe/package.json)); there is nothing to migrate. Workers Paid is **$5/month per account with 10M requests and 30M CPU-milliseconds included, and requests to static assets are free and unlimited** on both plans ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)), which is why a fleet of Astro storefronts costs almost nothing to serve. The deployment model, routing, fleet migrations, template pinning and upgrade jobs are specified in the engine document and are not repeated here ([engine-architecture.md §10](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)); what follows is the platform evidence those decisions rest on. One change from the earlier report: because the frontend is block-based from day one, with pages rendered server-side from validated block JSON and themes applied as CSS variables at render time, a content or theme change never needs a rebuild, and the per-tenant artifact is a D1 export plus a pinned template version rather than a per-client Astro project ([engine-architecture.md §5](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)).

### Three multi-tenant patterns, and why B now and C later

There are three ways to lay out the fleet. Pattern A, one shared multi-tenant Worker that resolves the tenant from the hostname and reads config from D1 or KV, is cheapest and pushes template updates fleet-wide with one deploy, but every client shares one blast radius, per-client code is impossible, and handoff means extracting a tenant into a standalone project. Pattern B, **one standard Worker per client site** on Workers Paid, gives each client its own Astro build, bindings, secrets and `workers.dev` preview URL; it is the natural fit for a developer already shipping Astro-on-Workers sites, and its hard caps are **500 Workers per account, 100 custom domains per zone, 1,000 routes per zone and 250 cron triggers per account** ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Pattern C puts each client site in a Workers for Platforms dispatch namespace: the WfP plan is **$25/month with 20M requests, 60M CPU-ms and 1,000 scripts included, then $0.02 per additional script** ([WfP pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/reference/pricing/)), "Cloudflare provides an unlimited number of scripts," scripts carry up to eight tags for customer metadata, the default cache is disabled for namespaced scripts, and user Workers cannot read `request.cf` unless the namespace runs in trusted mode ([WfP limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/reference/limits/)). A dispatch Worker routes each request with `env.DISPATCHER.get(name)` and `userWorker.fetch(request)` ([Dynamic dispatch](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/dynamic-dispatch/)), can impose per-tenant `limits: { cpuMs, subRequests }` ([Custom limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/platform/custom-limits/)), and an outbound Worker can log or restrict every `fetch()` a tenant makes ([Outbound Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/outbound-workers/)). Critically, the Astro build output is identical in B and C; the only difference is `npx wrangler deploy --name <site> --dispatch-namespace <ns>` instead of a plain deploy ([Static assets in WfP](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/static-assets/)). Start on B with a strict `site-<slug>` naming convention and move to C when you approach ~400 Workers, want per-tenant CPU caps, or ever run client-supplied code; the central scheduler that replaces per-tenant crons near 50 tenants is part of the same plan ([engine-architecture.md §4.12](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)).

Give every client its own D1. D1 "is designed for horizontal scale out across multiple, smaller (10 GB) databases, such as per-user, per-tenant or per-entity databases," with **50,000 databases per paid account, 10 GB each, 1 TB total**, and 5 GB of storage, 25 billion row reads and 50 million writes included in the $5 plan ([D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/); [D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)). A brochure or storefront database is tens of megabytes, so hundreds of tenants fit inside the included allowance; per-client export is a clean handoff artifact; and a bad migration breaks one client, not all. A shared D1 with `tenant_id` saves nothing and makes handoff worse, and the teardown prices it at 8–12 extra days because about 28 tables carry global uniqueness constraints. D1 supports SQLite's FTS5 for on-site search, but "export is not supported for virtual tables," so the handoff routine must drop FTS tables and rebuild them after import ([D1 SQL statements](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/sql-api/sql-statements/)); that matters for the parts store's thousand-SKU catalog, where search is an external-content FTS5 table rebuilt around exports.

### Preview URLs, go-live without an account move, and the apex trap

Every Worker gets a free `workers.dev` URL, and `wrangler versions upload --preview-alias demo` produces `demo-<worker>.<subdomain>.workers.dev` for throwaway revisions ([Workers preview URLs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/versions-and-deployments/preview-urls/), secondary). A branded `<slug>.preview.yourbrand.com` via Workers Custom Domains hits the 100-per-zone cap fast, and the docs' own advice is "if you require more than 100 custom domains per zone, consider using a wildcard route" ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)), so from day one route `*.preview.yourbrand.com/*` to a small router Worker that maps the subdomain to the site Worker via service binding (or, after moving to WfP, `env.DISPATCHER.get`). Gate previews with Cloudflare Access, which is free for up to 50 users with paid seats at about $7/user/month ([ControlD](https://controld.com/blog/cloudflare-zero-trust-pricing/), secondary), using the one-time-PIN identity provider so a prospect views a demo with just their email ([Access one-time PIN docs](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-one/integrations/identity-providers/one-time-pin.mdx)). The remote-first flow depends on this: the demo a prospect opens from a Loom link a thousand miles away is the same gated preview the founder would open on a laptop at the counter.

Go-live on the client's own domain is Cloudflare for SaaS. Free, Pro and Business zones all include **100 custom hostnames at no cost, then $0.10 per hostname per month, up to 50,000**; wildcard hostnames and custom certificates are Enterprise-only and apex proxying is a paid add-on ([Cloudflare for SaaS plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/plans/)). The flow is: set a fallback origin, have the client add a CNAME at their registrar pointing to your target (for example `sites.yourbrand.com`), create the hostname with `POST /zones/{zone_id}/custom_hostnames` specifying `ssl.method` of `http` or `txt`, and treat it as production-ready when `result.status` and `result.ssl.status` are both `active` ([Cloudflare for SaaS getting started](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/getting-started/)). To make a Worker the origin, create an originless `AAAA 100::` record and a `*/*` Worker route; note the caveat that when a route matches, the per-hostname `custom_origin_server` is bypassed, so hostname-to-tenant routing must happen inside your Worker from the Host header ([Worker as origin](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/advanced-settings/worker-as-origin/)). This is exactly the stack Cloudflare documents for WfP hostname routing, where the dispatch Worker looks up `hostname:<host>` in KV and dispatches to the mapped script ([Hostname routing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/hostname-routing/)). The sharp edge is the apex: `joespizza.com` without `www` only works via CNAME if the client's DNS host flattens at the apex, otherwise apex proxying is an Enterprise add-on, so make `www.` canonical, create the custom hostname for `www.<domain>`, and have the client set an apex redirect at their registrar, or move the client's DNS to a free Cloudflare zone, which flattens apex CNAMEs. Two mechanisms therefore exist for putting a client's domain in front of your Worker: add the client's domain as a zone in your account (full DNS control, tighter coupling) or leave DNS at their registrar and use a SaaS custom hostname (looser coupling, better for handoff, free for the first 100). Prefer the second for the managed tier; the single CNAME instruction is the only DNS step a remote owner ever sees.

### Handoff is redeploy, not transfer

Nothing in a Cloudflare account moves to another account as an object. Moving a domain means adding it as a new site in the target account and changing nameservers; "SSL/TLS certificates associated with your previous Cloudflare account will not be transferred," DNS records must be exported and re-imported, and "if your domain is registered with Cloudflare Registrar, you need to submit a manual request to transfer the domain and its registration to a new account" ([Move a domain between accounts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-domains/move-domain/)). D1 moves by `POST .../d1/database/{id}/export`, during which the database is unavailable for queries, then `wrangler d1 execute --file` on the other side ([D1 export API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/resources/d1/subresources/database/methods/export/)). Free, Pro and Business accounts have exactly two roles, one Super Administrator and unlimited Administrators who can do everything except billing and membership ([Manage account members](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-members/manage/), secondary). Because the pinned template version plus a generated `wrangler.jsonc` plus a D1 export is a complete reproduction of a site, "transfer" becomes a cheap, scripted redeploy if you plan for it from the start: one D1 per client, domains registered in the client's name (Cloudflare Registrar sells at cost, currently $10.46/year for .com (Verisign's $10.26 wholesale plus the $0.20 ICANN fee, same price for registration, renewal and transfer) and rising to about $11.17 when Verisign's announced wholesale increase to $10.97 takes effect on November 1, 2026, and allows transfer-out without penalty ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/cloudflare-becomes-a-registrar-sells-domains-at-cost/), secondary)), and nothing tenant-specific in your account that is not reproducible from the template and an export. This is also the honest answer to the reseller lock-in the competition section documents: "you can leave" is true because an exit is a CNAME change.

These estimates assume ~20,000 dynamic requests, ~5 ms CPU per request, ~30 MB D1 and ~200 MB R2 per site per month; a catalog-heavy retail site with search could be 5–10x the requests and still sit inside the included allowances. Domains at cost (~$10.50/year, ~$11.20 from November 2026) are the only per-site line item that is not fractions of a cent, and should be passed through. The gotchas are documented and mostly already solved in your stack. Cloudflare Email Service outbound sending is **Workers Paid only, 3,000 sends/month included then $0.35 per 1,000, and sends to verified destination addresses are free** ([Email Service pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/platform/pricing/)); MailChannels' free Workers API ended in 2024 ([MailChannels EOL](https://support.mailchannels.com/hc/en-us/articles/26814255454093-End-of-Life-Notice-Cloudflare-Workers)); and the `send_email` binding CatholicJoe uses ties outbound mail to a domain onboarded in the same account as the Worker ([docs/setup.md](/home/user/catholicjoe/docs/setup.md)), so under Model 1 either client DNS lives in your account or mail goes out from a founder-owned sending domain, which is why the engine makes the sender swappable and Resend the likely standard. Image transformations are free only up to 5,000 unique variants per month, then $0.50 per 1,000 ([Images pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/images/pricing/), secondary); CatholicJoe already uses `imageService: 'compile'` ([astro.config.mjs](/home/user/catholicjoe/astro.config.mjs)), which makes image cost zero at the expense of build time, so the engine keeps `compile` for template images and reserves the Images binding for owner uploads. Workers allow 6 simultaneous outbound connections per request and 128 MB per isolate, D1 caps bound parameters at 100 per query, and the adapter requires `nodejs_compat` with a compatibility date of 2024-09-23 or later ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); [Astro adapter docs](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare/)), so POS and marketplace feeds should be fetched by a job into KV or R2 rather than per request, and catalog imports must be chunked.

No alternative is cheaper or more automatable for hundreds of tiny sites with custom domains. Vercel Pro is $20 per seat with a $20 usage credit ([livemy](https://livemy.app/blog/vercel-pricing), secondary), which makes handing a project to a client a worse story than a free Cloudflare account; Netlify moved to a credit model in April 2026 ([netli.fyi](https://netli.fyi/blog/netlify-pricing-and-limits), secondary); Fly.io, Railway and a VPS running Coolify or Dokploy ([Introserv](https://introserv.com/blog/dokploy-vs-coolify-complete-comparison-of-the-best-self-hosted-paas-platforms-for-vps-and-dedicated-servers-2026/), secondary) win only when a site needs long-running processes, native Node modules or a real Postgres per client, and then you become the sysadmin. The real competitor is not a host but a platform the client may prefer to own: Hostinger WordPress at $2.99/month renewing at $10.99 ([WebsiteBuilderExpert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/web-hosting/hostinger-pricing/), secondary) wins when the owner insists on self-editing in a familiar CMS or on being able to fire you and hire any local WordPress freelancer. Cloudflare is the wrong choice in exactly those cases, plus three more: a relational database larger than 10 GB per tenant, an apex-only domain whose DNS host cannot flatten CNAMEs and whose owner will not move DNS, and newsletter-scale email volumes.

## The founder dashboard: a D1 CRM with a deploy button, fed by Overture and verified by Google

There are two admins, and this section covers the first. The founder dashboard is a separate Worker at `admin.<brand>.com` with its own platform D1, used by the founder and later a salesperson, never by clients; the tenant back-office lives under `/admin` inside each tenant's own Worker, grown from CatholicJoe's four admin pages, and gives the owner and their staff orders, products, hours, the block editor, inbox, bookings, the Apps page and settings, with a "platform access" switch the owner controls ([engine-architecture.md §8](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)). Nothing off the shelf runs on Workers and D1 except Payload CMS 3, whose official `@payloadcms/db-d1-sqlite` adapter is "currently in beta" and drags in a Next.js app ([Payload docs](https://github.com/payloadcms/payload/blob/main/docs/database/sqlite.mdx)); Twenty is the best open-source CRM UX but needs NestJS, PostgreSQL and Redis on a ~2 GB VPS ([twenty README](https://github.com/twentyhq/twenty); [ossalt](https://ossalt.com/guides/how-to-self-host-twenty-crm-hubspot-alternative-2026)); NocoDB moved to a Sustainable Use License in January 2026 ([NocoDB license](https://nocodb.com/docs/self-hosting/license)) and Directus's repository now carries a Monospace Sustainable Core License ([directus license](https://github.com/directus/directus/blob/main/license)), both still fine for internal use but both Node servers; and Attio's free plan (3 seats) or HubSpot's free CRM ([MarketBetter](https://marketbetter.ai/blog/attio-crm-pricing-breakdown-2026/); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-crm-software)) cannot host the "Deploy this site" button or the per-site Cloudflare state without a second integration layer, so you would double-enter. ### Schema, pipeline and identity resolution

Model the CRM around an `accounts` table (the business, with `kind` of local, etsy, amazon, ebay, webstore or mixed, normalized name, E.164 phone, email domain, `website_tech`, `source`, `source_license`, `source_release`, `source_record_id`, `presence`, `verify_result`, `stripe_customer_id` and `do_not_contact`), with many `channel_listings` rows as the dedup backbone (one per Google place, Overture row, Etsy shop, Amazon storefront, eBay username or website, `UNIQUE(channel, external_id)`, with `metrics_json` for ratings, listing and sales counts), `contacts` with explicit `consent_email` and `consent_sms` flags and `unsubscribed_at`, a single open `deals` row per account carrying the pipeline stage (`lead`, `demo_built`, `demo_sent`, `call`, `paid`, `live`, `managed`, `handed_off`, `lost`) with `next_action_at`, `plan`, `mrr_cents` and `setup_fee_cents`, an append-only `activities` log, and the Cloudflare mirror: `sites` (kind demo or production, `template` and `template_version`, `schema_version`, `dispatch_namespace`, `script_name`, `preview_hostname`, `custom_hostname` and `custom_hostname_id`, `hostname_status`, `ssl_status`, `d1_database_id`, `kv_namespace_id`, `r2_bucket`, `status` of draft, building, preview, live, suspended or deleted, `noindex`, `access_protected`), `deploy_jobs` (action build_demo, deploy, migrate, upgrade, go_live, attach_domain, rotate_secret, suspend or delete, with `workflow_instance_id`, payload and result), plus `subscriptions` and `invoices` mirrored from your own Stripe, which is never mixed with a tenant's Stripe. Enforce the pipeline in code: `demo_built` requires a `sites` row in `preview`, `demo_sent` requires an outbound activity with a consent check, `paid` requires an active subscription written by the Stripe webhook rather than by hand, and `live` requires both hostname and SSL status `active`. Resolve identity across sources in order: exact channel and external id, E.164 phone, website host or email domain, normalized name plus postal code, and finally an FTS5 prefix match within ~500 m surfaced as a "possible duplicate" card that a human confirms; never auto-merge on the fuzzy rule. D1 supports FTS5 and JSON ([D1 supported extensions](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/partials/d1/d1-do-supported-sqlite-extensions.mdx)), so use one external-content FTS5 table over accounts and contacts maintained by triggers and ranked with `bm25()`, fall back to `LIKE` on name and phone for short numeric input while respecting D1's **50-byte LIKE pattern limit and 100 bound parameters per query** ([D1 limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/d1/platform/limits.mdx)), and drop and recreate the index around exports because databases with virtual tables cannot be exported ([zenn.dev](https://zenn.dev/cybozu_frontend/articles/cloudflare-d1-fts)). A weekly hygiene Workflow nulls Google-sourced `lat`/`lng` older than 30 days, re-checks `do_not_contact`, expires rows that failed verification twice and pulls the last week of Florida filings. Paginate with keyset cursors rather than OFFSET, render tables server-side in Astro with sort and filter in the query string, and hydrate a single React island with TanStack Table's `manualPagination` and `manualSorting` ([TanStack Table](https://tanstack.com/table/latest/docs/framework/react/guide/pagination)) only for the kanban and inline-edit grids.

### The deploy button: a Workflow that builds, uploads, routes and polls

An admin action inserts a `deploy_jobs` row and starts a Cloudflare Workflow instance; Workflows are included in Workers Free and Paid, allow 30 s of CPU per step on Paid (configurable to 5 min), up to 10,000 steps, and `step.sleep` up to 365 days, which is what lets a go-live job wait days for a client to fix DNS ([Workflows limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workflows/reference/limits.mdx); [Workflows pricing](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workflows/reference/pricing.mdx)). The one hard constraint is that `astro build` cannot run inside a Worker, so the bundle step fetches a prebuilt template bundle for `template@version` from R2 (build once per template version in CI, since per-client differences are bindings, vars and block JSON in the site's own D1) or triggers a build runner: Workers Builds includes 6,000 build minutes a month on Paid at $0.005 per extra minute ([Workers Builds](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/ci-cd/builds/limits-and-pricing/)), or a GitHub Actions `workflow_dispatch` running `cloudflare/wrangler-action@v3` with `deploy --name site-<slug>` ([rwsdk](https://rwsdk.com/blog/zero-drama-cloudflare-workers-github-actions), secondary). The upload step calls `PUT /accounts/{account_id}/workers/dispatch/namespaces/{ns}/scripts/{script_name}` as multipart with a `metadata` JSON carrying `main_module`, `bindings`, `tags` and `compatibility_date`, with static assets uploaded first via an assets-upload-session ([WfP platform examples](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/reference/platform-examples.mdx)); bindings must be in that metadata, each user Worker sees only what is explicitly attached, so every site gets its own D1, KV and R2 ([WfP bindings](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/bindings.mdx)); tags like `account-<id>` and `demo` or `prod` enable `DELETE .../scripts?tags=account-<id>:yes` for a one-click teardown ([WfP tags](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/tags.mdx)). On Pattern B the equivalent is a plain `wrangler deploy --name site-<slug>` from the runner with a generated `wrangler.jsonc`. The route step writes `hostname:<slug>.preview.yourbrand.com → site-<slug>` to the routing KV; the go-live step POSTs the custom hostname with `ssl.method: "http"` and polls GET until status and `ssl.status` are `active`, with intermediate `ssl.status` values of `initializing`, `pending_validation`, `pending_issuance` and `pending_deployment` ([Custom hostnames create](https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/create), secondary) and hostname states including Pending, Active, Blocked and Moved ([Validation status](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/domain-support/hostname-validation/validation-status.mdx)). Each step writes back to `deploy_jobs.result_json` and `sites`, and the site detail page shows `script_name`, preview URL, custom hostname with status chips, a "Re-check" button, and the last five jobs. The same job runner carries the `upgrade` action that re-validates every published page against a new block schema, applies migrations and rolls back on a failed smoke test, so the fleet moves as waves rather than as hand work ([engine-architecture.md §10](/home/user/consultingTemplate/docs/engine-architecture.md)).

Token and secret design: one account-scoped API token used only by the Workflow Worker, with Workers Scripts, Workers KV Storage, D1 and R2 Write on the account and SSL and Certificates plus DNS Write on the SaaS zone only ([API token permissions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/reference/permissions/)), stored as a Worker secret, never a Global API Key. Per-client Stripe, ShipStation and Mailchimp credentials belong in the site Worker's own secret bindings (64 variables on Free, 128 on Paid, 5 KB each ([Workers limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workers/platform/limits.mdx))), set by a provisioning job and never by a browser action, with only non-secret references in D1; Secrets Store, still in beta with about 100 secrets per account, suits shared platform keys rather than per-client ones ([Secrets Store Workers integration](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/secrets-store/integrations/workers.mdx)). Protect the dashboard in layers: a Cloudflare Access application on `admin.yourbrand.com` with Include = your emails and Require = your IP ranges or Country = US, so the login page itself is invisible to the internet ([Access policies](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-one/access-controls/policies/index.mdx)); better-auth's passkey plugin, powered by SimpleWebAuthn, as the primary factor ([better-auth passkey docs](https://github.com/better-auth/better-auth/blob/main/docs/content/docs/plugins/passkey.mdx)); the admin plugin's role check in middleware for every `/admin/*` route; and an Access service token for the Workflow and cron callers. "Open tenant back-office" from the fleet screen works only while that tenant's owner-controlled platform-access switch is on, and every such session is audited in the tenant's own `audit_events`, the generalization of CatholicJoe's developer-access switch. Cloudflare's own "internal static sites platform" template, which puts every published site behind Access on WfP, is the closest reference implementation for a demo-site publisher ([WfP get started](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/get-started.mdx)).

### Outreach and demo-site legality baked into the pipeline

Cold B2B email is legal under CAN-SPAM's opt-out model, with no B2B exemption: accurate headers, an honest subject line, identification as an ad, a physical postal address, a one-click opt-out honored within 10 business days, and penalties of **up to $53,088 per email** ([EmailListValidation](https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/does-canspam-apply-to-b2b-cold-email-outreach/)). Cold texts or autodialed calls to any mobile number need prior express consent under the TCPA at $500–$1,500 per violation, and a business mobile is treated like a personal one absent a prior relationship ([Wipfli](https://www.wipfli.com/insights/articles/does-tcpa-apply-to-b2b-marketing-key-rules)), so SMS is not an outreach channel for numbers found in Overture or Google data; a founder-dialled call to a business line is outside the autodialer rules; the email template should carry your address and an unsubscribe link that flips `do_not_contact`, and every send should run a suppression check. State mini-TCPA statutes, Florida's Telephone Solicitation Act among them, were not researched and should be checked before a Florida-heavy campaign. For the demo sites themselves, the defense for using a business's name is nominative fair use, whose three factors are that the service is not readily identifiable without the mark, only as much of the mark as necessary is used, and nothing suggests sponsorship or endorsement ([Playboy v. Welles](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1233126.html)). Translate that into practice, and into preset defaults rather than founder discipline: serve demos on `<slug>.preview.yourbrand.com` with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`; gate them behind Access OTP or a signed link; use the business name in plain text only, never the logo or brand photography; use your own placeholder imagery and a generic sample menu or catalog, never pasted Google or Yelp reviews or their site's text, since Google's terms specifically forbid copying reviews ([Google Maps Platform Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)) and photo copyright belongs to whoever took them; show a persistent "Unsolicited concept by <you>; not affiliated with or endorsed by <business>" banner; and delete the Worker and hostname immediately on request, logging the takedown in the CRM. Because demos are generated by substitution from the preset, the more than 90% of them that never convert cost minutes, which is the only condition under which a $0-down, demo-first motion survives the 0.37% cold-email baseline.

## What the sandbox could not verify, and how to check it yourself

Three classes of claim in this report are unverified and must be re-checked by you before money changes hands. Run the following from any unrestricted machine before buying a domain or filing a mark; it checks .com via Verisign's RDAP, the alt-TLDs via the rdap.org bootstrap redirector (which answers 302 + Location, so curl must follow with -L), the suffix forms, and the GitHub and npm handles, where **404 from the authoritative server means unregistered or free, 200 means registered (possibly parked), and a 404 that stays on rdap.org means that TLD has no IANA-registered RDAP server, not that the name is free**.

```bash
# usage: bash verify.sh statio shopstead pundak waystead mansio mercantry
for n in "$@"; do
  for tld in com co io app store shop; do
    case $tld in com) u="https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/$n.$tld";; *) u="https://rdap.org/domain/$n.$tld";; esac
    printf "%-14s .%-5s -> %s\n" "$n" "$tld" "$(curl -sL -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code} %{url_effective}' "$u")"   # 200 = registered (maybe parked), 404 = unregistered ONLY if url_effective is no longer rdap.org; a 404 still at rdap.org means rdap.org knows no RDAP server for that TLD (IANA bootstrap gap, reported for .io) — query the registry's RDAP directly (e.g. https://rdap.identitydigital.services/rdap/domain/NAME.io); 302 means -L was dropped
  done
  for v in get$n try$n ${n}hq ${n}commerce; do
    printf "%-14s .com   -> %s\n" "$v" "$(curl -sL -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code} %{url_effective}' "https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/$v.com")"
  done
  printf "%-14s github -> %s\n" "$n" "$(curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' "https://api.github.com/users/$n")"      # 404 = free
  printf "%-14s npm    -> %s\n" "$n" "$(curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' "https://registry.npmjs.org/$n")"    # 404 = free
done
# Then: https://tmsearch.uspto.gov (live marks, classes 9/35/42), crunchbase.com, producthunt.com, apps.apple.com for the top 3.
```

## Conclusion

The research reframes the venture in three ways. First, the founder's vision and the evidence converge on the same shape from opposite directions: the vision wants one engine, a remote-first operation and an owner who never handles a key, and the viability arithmetic wants maintenance under an hour per site, a demo that costs minutes and integrations the owner cannot easily take elsewhere. Those are the same requirement. The block-based frontend, the preset scaffold and the connection records are not platform ambition; they are the mechanism by which sixty subscriptions fit inside one person's month, and they happen to be the substrate a conversational editor needs later. The extra three to four weeks between 44–60 and 65–91 days buy exactly the things that let the business run without the founder standing at the counter.

Second, discovery has turned from a legal puzzle into a measured pipeline: one SQL statement over a public bucket returns a metro's website-less businesses with their licenses attached, Google verifies one row at a time within its terms, and state filings add timing, so the constraint has moved from finding prospects to converting them. Third, the competition analysis hands the founder a position no incumbent can copy cheaply. The resellers have the sales forces and the one-star reviews; the AI builders have the price and leave the owner alone; the vertical platforms own the workflow and want nothing to do with websites. "It is done, it is wired to your tools, you own it, you can leave" is defensible because the engine makes leaving an export and a CNAME change, and because a solo founder on a $5-a-month platform can afford to say it. The name remains the one decision that should wait on data you do not yet have: run the script, search the marks, then commit.
