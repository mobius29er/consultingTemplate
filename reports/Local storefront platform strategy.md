## Productized service first, with marketplace sellers as the wedge

The three directions you listed are not peers; they are stages, and the public record says the order is service, then managed platform, and never self-serve SaaS. The margin map comes from the two biggest comparables. Shopify's blended gross margin was **47.7%** in Q2 2026, dragged down by payments revenue that grew 37% to $2.78B against subscription revenue of $802M ([Shopify 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001594805/000159480526000047/shop-20260630.htm)); Wix reports **80% gross margin on Creative Subscriptions but 33% on Business Solutions** ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Wix+Reports+Second+Quarter+2026+Results/26859973.html)). Keep your revenue in software and service subscriptions and leave payments, fraud and chargebacks with Stripe on the client's own account via Stripe Connect Standard, which is free and leaves the client as merchant of record, versus $2 per active account plus 0.25% and $0.25 per payout for Express or Custom ([paymentcloudinc](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/stripe-connect/)). The self-serve horizontal graveyard is the other half of the argument: Volusion's live stores fell roughly **74%** from about 13,889 to about 3,575 after its 2020 bankruptcy ([kensium](https://www.kensium.com/blog/ecommerce-options-for-volusion-customers-post-bankruptcy)), Tictail was acquired by Shopify and closed ([ecommercebytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2018/11/22/tictail-to-close-after-shopify-acquires-the-marketplace/)), and Selz was bought by Amazon and shut down ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/companies/latest-news-on-Selz--26509)). The entrants that worked were vertical and sales-led, above all Owner.com, which raised **$240M at a $2.3B valuation in August 2026 with more than $100M ARR** ([Goldman Sachs press release](https://am.gs.com/en-ae/advisors/news/press-release/2026/owner-raises-240m-gs-alts)) on $249–$499/month plans ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees)), or niche with free entry (Fourthwall, Big Cartel, Gumroad), which a solo founder cannot fund. GoHighLevel's $497/month "SaaS Mode" and Duda's $149/month white-label tier show agencies pay $150–$500/month just to operate a multi-tenant platform ([webdew](https://www.webdew.com/blog/gohighlevel-saas-mode); [joinsecret](https://www.joinsecret.com/duda/pricing)); you already own that layer, which is the structural reason the managed platform (B) is attractive once the service (A) proves demand.

### Sellers are the product; local service businesses are the cash

The seller segment is the one your reference build already serves, and its economics are quantifiable. Etsy had **5.6M active sellers** and $10.46B of GMS in 2025 ([Etsy Q4 2025 release](https://investors.etsy.com/_assets/_fea57334fe81a735b34cf3bf4bdcb553/etsy/db/938/10062/earnings_release/Exhibit+99.1+12.31.2025.pdf)); **89% are one-person businesses and 97% run from home** ([ecommercebytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/?p=227358)). A US seller pays $0.20 per listing, 6.5% transaction fee and 3% + $0.25 processing, roughly 10.6% effective, plus a **mandatory 12% Offsite Ads fee above $10,000/year** ([edesk](https://www.edesk.com/blog/etsy-seller-fees)); Amazon takes 15% referral in most categories plus $39.99/month ([repricer](https://www.repricer.com/?p=1593); [feedvisor](https://feedvisor.com/university/referral-fee/)); eBay takes 13.6% plus $0.30–$0.40 per order without a Store ([eBay Seller Center](https://www.ebay.com/sellercenter/resources/seller-updates/2025-january/final-value-fee); [taxomate](https://taxomate.com/blog/ebay-seller-fees)). The fee math sets your price ceiling: diverting 25% of a $5,000/month Etsy shop to a Stripe store saves only **$87–$117/month net of Stripe fees** (computed from the schedules above at $40 AOV), so a flat fee must stay under about $50 for the arithmetic alone to work, and the stronger pitch is owning the buyer list and escaping Offsite Ads and algorithm risk. Amazon and eBay sellers save about 60% more per diverted dollar ($137–$142), and 21% of Amazon-based businesses already ran Shopify with 35% planning to add it ([homepagenews](https://homepagenews.com/retail-articles/jungle-scout-amazon-based-business-see-opportunities-elsewhere/)), but Amazon controls the buyer relationship and eBay sellers are often one-off resellers, so Etsy and small eBay shops are the easier first conversions.

Local service businesses are the higher-ticket lane. Clutch's August 2025 survey found **83% of US small businesses have a website, so 17% are offline, and cost as the top objection fell to 16%** ([Clutch via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250821848152/en/Clutch-Report-No-Code-Tools-Fuel-Website-Growth-Yet-17-of-Small-Businesses-are-Still-Offline)); other surveys put non-adoption at 27–29% ([review42](https://resources.review42.com/what-percentage-of-small-businesses-have-a-website/)). The market price is established: freelancers charge $1,500–$5,000 and agencies $5,000–$15,000 for a 5–10 page site ([websitesetup](https://websitesetup.org/how-much-does-a-website-cost/)), care plans run $50–$500/month ([apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost)), and "$99/month, $0 upfront, 12-month minimum" is a widely advertised website-as-a-service offer ([redefineweb](https://redefineweb.com/services/pay-monthly-websites-design-smb/); [Digital Main Street](https://digitalmainstreet.ca/product/simple-solution-e-commerce/)). The Clutch data also tells you what the $0-down demo removes: effort and risk objections, not price objections, so the demo must be good enough to make doing nothing feel costly.

Pizza shops are the segment to defer even though they headline your brief. **72% of restaurant operators already have a website and 67% of consumers prefer ordering from a restaurant's own site** ([restolabs](https://restolabs.com/blog/online-ordering-statistics-every-restaurateur-should-know)), which sounds like demand, but the money is in ordering: ChowNow charges $229–$449/month plus $119–$499 setup ([ChowNow pricing](https://get.chownow.com/pricing/)), Slice takes 5% of sales ([Slice pricing](https://slice.com/pricing)), Square for Restaurants starts at $0 ([upmenu](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/square-pricing)) and DoorDash Storefront is commission-free at $0/month ([getsauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/doordash-fees-for-restaurants-2)). A static Astro + Stripe Checkout site does not do modifiers, kitchen printing or POS sync, so the only honest offer to a pizzeria is a brochure plus menu plus a link to Square or DoorDash ordering, which competes with free. Take those deals when they walk in; do not prospect for them.

### The demo-first motion only works if demo generation is automated

The evidence favors personalized, pre-built demos over pitches, but the baseline is brutal. An 820-email test to small businesses produced **3 replies (0.37%)**, a "free demo" offer converted better than any pricing discussion, and referencing the prospect's specific site problems lifted replies 3–5x ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/800-cold-emails-later-heres-what-actually-moves-the-needle-and-what-s-a-complete-waste-of-time-1e67d7e295)); average cold-email reply rates are 1–5% ([folderly](https://generate.folderly.com/blog/cold-email-statistics-2025)); one practitioner reports 15% replies by screen-sharing the prospect's site in a Loom ([RevGenius](https://community.revgenius.com/members/4df870b1-30f2-4606-8730-80dafdf9a94f)). A realistic solo funnel is 100 personalized demos to 5–15 replies to 2–5 calls to 1–2 closes, so at 1–3 hours per hand-built demo you would spend 100–300 hours per client; the templated, data-driven demo generator is therefore the real product, not a nice-to-have. AIGA formally discourages speculative work for wasted hours and IP ambiguity ([boostlabs](https://boostlabs.com/blog/design-spec-work/)), which argues for contracting around it: watermarked preview URLs, `noindex`, 7–14 day auto-expiry, and a visible "unsolicited concept, not affiliated" banner (the legal basis is in the dashboard section). On the founder's own invoices, note that SaaS and web services are untaxed in some states (Georgia, for example, taxes neither SaaS nor custom software ([numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/saas-sales-tax-georgia))) but many states do tax SaaS; your state was not given and must be checked. Standard agency subscription contracts keep template IP with the agency under a license, give 30-day notice after a 6–12 month minimum, and assign the domain and content to the client ([Magellan Design T&Cs](https://magellandesign.com/standard-terms-and-conditions-of-business)); do the same, and make "you own your domain, Stripe, list and content from day one" the headline term.

## Cloudflare is right: one Worker and one D1 per client, $5 a month

Cloudflare is the correct platform, and the reason is as much about direction of travel as about price. The Pages documentation now carries the banner "Workers supports most Pages use cases and offers a broader feature set. It is Cloudflare's primary platform for building applications. Start new projects with Workers" ([Cloudflare Pages docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/)); the migration guide lists Cron Triggers, Durable Objects, Email Workers, Queue consumers, Gradual Deployments and Logpush as Workers-only ([Migrate from Pages](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/migration-guides/migrate-from-pages/)); and the Astro adapter "deploys to Cloudflare Workers only. Support for Cloudflare Pages has been removed," with Astro 6 moving runtime access to `import { env } from 'cloudflare:workers'` ([Astro Cloudflare adapter docs](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare/)). CatholicJoe already runs Astro 7.3.4 with `@astrojs/cloudflare` 14.3.3, static assets and `wrangler deploy` ([package.json](/home/user/catholicjoe/package.json)); there is nothing to migrate. Workers Paid is **$5/month per account with 10M requests and 30M CPU-milliseconds included, and requests to static assets are free and unlimited** on both plans ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)), which is why a fleet of prerendered Astro storefronts costs almost nothing to serve.

### Three multi-tenant patterns, and why B now and C later

There are three ways to lay out the fleet. Pattern A, one shared multi-tenant Worker that resolves the tenant from the hostname and reads config from D1 or KV, is cheapest and pushes template updates fleet-wide with one deploy, but every client shares one blast radius, per-client code is impossible, and handoff means extracting a tenant into a standalone project. Pattern B, **one standard Worker per client site** on Workers Paid, gives each client its own Astro build, bindings, secrets and `workers.dev` preview URL; it is the natural fit for a developer already shipping Astro-on-Workers sites, and its hard caps are **500 Workers per account, 100 custom domains per zone, 1,000 routes per zone and 250 cron triggers per account** ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Pattern C puts each client site in a Workers for Platforms dispatch namespace: the WfP plan is **$25/month with 20M requests, 60M CPU-ms and 1,000 scripts included, then $0.02 per additional script** ([WfP pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/reference/pricing/)), "Cloudflare provides an unlimited number of scripts," scripts carry up to eight tags for customer metadata, the default cache is disabled for namespaced scripts, and user Workers cannot read `request.cf` unless the namespace runs in trusted mode ([WfP limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/reference/limits/)). A dispatch Worker routes each request with `env.DISPATCHER.get(name)` and `userWorker.fetch(request)` ([Dynamic dispatch](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/dynamic-dispatch/)), can impose per-tenant `limits: { cpuMs, subRequests }` ([Custom limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/platform/custom-limits/)), and an outbound Worker can log or restrict every `fetch()` a tenant makes ([Outbound Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/outbound-workers/)). Critically, the Astro build output is identical in B and C; the only difference is `npx wrangler deploy --name <site> --dispatch-namespace <ns>` instead of a plain deploy ([Static assets in WfP](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/static-assets/)). Start on B with a strict `site-<slug>` naming convention and move to C when you approach ~400 Workers, want per-tenant CPU caps, or ever run client-supplied code.

Give every client its own D1. D1 "is designed for horizontal scale out across multiple, smaller (10 GB) databases, such as per-user, per-tenant or per-entity databases," with **50,000 databases per paid account, 10 GB each, 1 TB total**, and 5 GB of storage, 25 billion row reads and 50 million writes included in the $5 plan ([D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/); [D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)). A brochure or storefront database is tens of megabytes, so hundreds of tenants fit inside the included allowance; per-client export is a clean handoff artifact; and a bad migration breaks one client, not all. A shared D1 with `tenant_id` saves nothing and makes handoff worse. D1 supports SQLite's FTS5 for on-site search, but "export is not supported for virtual tables," so the handoff routine must drop FTS tables and rebuild them after import ([D1 SQL statements](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/sql-api/sql-statements/)).

### Preview URLs, go-live without an account move, and the apex trap

Every Worker gets a free `workers.dev` URL, and `wrangler versions upload --preview-alias demo` produces `demo-<worker>.<subdomain>.workers.dev` for throwaway revisions ([Workers preview URLs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/versions-and-deployments/preview-urls/), secondary). A branded `<slug>.preview.yourbrand.com` via Workers Custom Domains hits the 100-per-zone cap fast, and the docs' own advice is "if you require more than 100 custom domains per zone, consider using a wildcard route" ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)), so from day one route `*.preview.yourbrand.com/*` to a small router Worker that maps the subdomain to the site Worker via service binding (or, after moving to WfP, `env.DISPATCHER.get`). Gate previews with Cloudflare Access, which is free for up to 50 users with paid seats at about $7/user/month ([ControlD](https://controld.com/blog/cloudflare-zero-trust-pricing/), secondary), using the one-time-PIN identity provider so a prospect views a demo with just their email ([Access one-time PIN docs](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-one/integrations/identity-providers/one-time-pin.mdx)).

Go-live on the client's own domain is Cloudflare for SaaS. Free, Pro and Business zones all include **100 custom hostnames at no cost, then $0.10 per hostname per month, up to 50,000**; wildcard hostnames and custom certificates are Enterprise-only and apex proxying is a paid add-on ([Cloudflare for SaaS plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/plans/)). The flow is: set a fallback origin, have the client add a CNAME at their registrar pointing to your target (for example `sites.yourbrand.com`), create the hostname with `POST /zones/{zone_id}/custom_hostnames` specifying `ssl.method` of `http` or `txt`, and treat it as production-ready when `result.status` and `result.ssl.status` are both `active` ([Cloudflare for SaaS getting started](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/getting-started/)). To make a Worker the origin, create an originless `AAAA 100::` record and a `*/*` Worker route; note the caveat that when a route matches, the per-hostname `custom_origin_server` is bypassed, so hostname-to-tenant routing must happen inside your Worker from the Host header ([Worker as origin](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/advanced-settings/worker-as-origin/)). This is exactly the stack Cloudflare documents for WfP hostname routing, where the dispatch Worker looks up `hostname:<host>` in KV and dispatches to the mapped script ([Hostname routing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/hostname-routing/)). The sharp edge is the apex: `joespizza.com` without `www` only works via CNAME if the client's DNS host flattens at the apex, otherwise apex proxying is an Enterprise add-on, so make `www.` canonical, create the custom hostname for `www.<domain>`, and have the client set an apex redirect at their registrar, or move the client's DNS to a free Cloudflare zone, which flattens apex CNAMEs. Two mechanisms therefore exist for putting a client's domain in front of your Worker: add the client's domain as a zone in your account (full DNS control, tighter coupling) or leave DNS at their registrar and use a SaaS custom hostname (looser coupling, better for handoff, free for the first 100). Prefer the second for the managed tier.

### Handoff is redeploy, not transfer

Nothing in a Cloudflare account moves to another account as an object. Moving a domain means adding it as a new site in the target account and changing nameservers; "SSL/TLS certificates associated with your previous Cloudflare account will not be transferred," DNS records must be exported and re-imported, and "if your domain is registered with Cloudflare Registrar, you need to submit a manual request to transfer the domain and its registration to a new account" ([Move a domain between accounts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-domains/move-domain/)). D1 moves by `POST .../d1/database/{id}/export`, during which the database is unavailable for queries, then `wrangler d1 execute --file` on the other side ([D1 export API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/resources/d1/subresources/database/methods/export/)). Free, Pro and Business accounts have exactly two roles, one Super Administrator and unlimited Administrators who can do everything except billing and membership ([Manage account members](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-members/manage/), secondary). Because the Astro repo plus `wrangler.jsonc` plus a D1 export is a complete reproduction of a site, "transfer" becomes a cheap, scripted redeploy if you plan for it from the start: one D1 per client, domains registered in the client's name (Cloudflare Registrar sells at cost, about $9–$10/year for .com, and allows transfer-out without penalty ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/cloudflare-becomes-a-registrar-sells-domains-at-cost/), secondary)), and nothing tenant-specific in your account that is not reproducible from the repo and an export.

| Handoff model | Where it lives | Procedure | Effort | Ongoing burden | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Managed on your account | Your account; client domain via SaaS custom hostname or client zone added to your account | Create hostname, send CNAME instruction, poll SSL | ~15 min | You own uptime, edits, Stripe, email deliverability; $0 per site to 100 hostnames | Monthly-managed tier; exit is "change the CNAME" |
| 2. Deployed into the client's own account | Client's free Cloudflare account; you are an Administrator | Client adds domain and invites you; you create a scoped token (Workers Scripts, KV, D1, R2, SSL, Routes, DNS Write), `wrangler d1 create` + import, `secret put`, `wrangler deploy`, attach custom domain; client adds a card for Workers Paid ($5) if the site needs it | 1–2 h first time, ~30 min scripted | Shared; you can deploy fixes but billing, 2FA and recovery are theirs | Buy-out clients who want ownership but not the keys |
| 3. Full handoff | Client's account only | Transfer the Git repo, deliver D1 export, secrets list and runbook; make them Super Administrator; revoke your membership and tokens | 2–4 h | Zero (sell a support retainer if wanted) | Exits; avoid ever buying their domain in your account |

These estimates assume ~20,000 dynamic requests, ~5 ms CPU per request, ~30 MB D1 and ~200 MB R2 per site per month; a catalog-heavy seller site with search could be 5–10x the requests and still sit inside the included allowances. Domains at cost (~$10/year) are the only per-site line item that is not fractions of a cent, and should be passed through. The gotchas are documented and mostly already solved in your stack. Cloudflare Email Service outbound sending is **Workers Paid only, 3,000 sends/month included then $0.35 per 1,000, and sends to verified destination addresses are free** ([Email Service pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/platform/pricing/)); MailChannels' free Workers API ended in 2024 ([MailChannels EOL](https://support.mailchannels.com/hc/en-us/articles/26814255454093-End-of-Life-Notice-Cloudflare-Workers)); and the `send_email` binding CatholicJoe uses ties outbound mail to a domain onboarded in the same account as the Worker ([docs/setup.md](/home/user/catholicjoe/docs/setup.md)), so under Model 1 either client DNS lives in your account or mail goes out from a founder-owned sending domain, and sites that email customers heavily should use Resend or another HTTPS ESP. Image transformations are free only up to 5,000 unique variants per month, then $0.50 per 1,000 ([Images pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/images/pricing/), secondary); CatholicJoe already uses `imageService: 'compile'` ([astro.config.mjs](/home/user/catholicjoe/astro.config.mjs)), which makes image cost zero at the expense of build time, and that should be the template default. Workers allow 6 simultaneous outbound connections per request and 128 MB per isolate, D1 caps bound parameters at 100 per query, and the adapter requires `nodejs_compat` with a compatibility date of 2024-09-23 or later ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); [Astro adapter docs](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare/)), so marketplace feeds should be fetched by a cron into KV or R2 rather than per request, and catalog imports must be chunked.

No alternative is cheaper or more automatable for hundreds of tiny sites with custom domains. Vercel Pro is $20 per seat with a $20 usage credit ([livemy](https://livemy.app/blog/vercel-pricing), secondary), which makes handing a project to a client a worse story than a free Cloudflare account; Netlify moved to a credit model in April 2026 ([netli.fyi](https://netli.fyi/blog/netlify-pricing-and-limits), secondary); Fly.io, Railway and a VPS running Coolify or Dokploy ([Introserv](https://introserv.com/blog/dokploy-vs-coolify-complete-comparison-of-the-best-self-hosted-paas-platforms-for-vps-and-dedicated-servers-2026/), secondary) win only when a site needs long-running processes, native Node modules or a real Postgres per client, and then you become the sysadmin. The real competitor is not a host but a platform the client may prefer to own: Hostinger WordPress at $2.99/month renewing at $10.99 ([WebsiteBuilderExpert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/web-hosting/hostinger-pricing/), secondary) wins when the owner insists on self-editing in a familiar CMS or on being able to fire you and hire any local WordPress freelancer. Cloudflare is the wrong choice in exactly those cases, plus three more: a relational database larger than 10 GB per tenant, an apex-only domain whose DNS host cannot flatten CNAMEs and whose owner will not move DNS, and newsletter-scale email volumes.

### Schema, pipeline and identity resolution

Model the CRM around an `accounts` table (the business, with `kind` of local, etsy, amazon, ebay, webstore or mixed, normalized name, E.164 phone, email domain, `website_tech`, `source`, `stripe_customer_id` and `do_not_contact`), with many `channel_listings` rows as the dedup backbone (one per Google place, FSQ row, Etsy shop, Amazon storefront, eBay username or website, `UNIQUE(channel, external_id)`, with `metrics_json` for ratings, listing and sales counts), `contacts` with explicit `consent_email` and `consent_sms` flags and `unsubscribed_at`, a single open `deals` row per account carrying the pipeline stage (`lead`, `demo_built`, `demo_sent`, `call`, `paid`, `live`, `managed`, `handed_off`, `lost`) with `next_action_at`, `plan`, `mrr_cents` and `setup_fee_cents`, an append-only `activities` log, and the Cloudflare mirror: `sites` (kind demo or production, `template` and `template_version`, `dispatch_namespace`, `script_name`, `preview_hostname`, `custom_hostname` and `custom_hostname_id`, `hostname_status`, `ssl_status`, `d1_database_id`, `kv_namespace_id`, `r2_bucket`, `status` of draft, building, preview, live, suspended or deleted, `noindex`, `access_protected`), `deploy_jobs` (action build_demo, deploy, go_live, attach_domain, suspend or delete, with `workflow_instance_id`, payload and result), plus `subscriptions` and `invoices` mirrored from your own Stripe. Enforce the pipeline in code: `demo_built` requires a `sites` row in `preview`, `demo_sent` requires an outbound activity with a consent check, `paid` requires an active subscription written by the Stripe webhook rather than by hand, and `live` requires both hostname and SSL status `active`. Resolve identity across sources in order: exact channel and external id, E.164 phone, website host or email domain, normalized name plus postal code, and finally an FTS5 prefix match within ~500 m surfaced as a "possible duplicate" card that a human confirms; never auto-merge on the fuzzy rule. D1 supports FTS5 and JSON ([D1 supported extensions](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/partials/d1/d1-do-supported-sqlite-extensions.mdx)), so use one external-content FTS5 table over accounts and contacts maintained by triggers and ranked with `bm25()`, fall back to `LIKE` on name and phone for short numeric input while respecting D1's **50-byte LIKE pattern limit and 100 bound parameters per query** ([D1 limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/d1/platform/limits.mdx)), and drop and recreate the index around exports because databases with virtual tables cannot be exported ([zenn.dev](https://zenn.dev/cybozu_frontend/articles/cloudflare-d1-fts)). A nightly cron nulls Google-sourced `lat`/`lng` older than 30 days. Paginate with keyset cursors rather than OFFSET, render tables server-side in Astro with sort and filter in the query string, and hydrate a single React island with TanStack Table's `manualPagination` and `manualSorting` ([TanStack Table](https://tanstack.com/table/latest/docs/framework/react/guide/pagination)) only for the kanban and inline-edit grids.

### The deploy button: a Workflow that builds, uploads, routes and polls

An admin action inserts a `deploy_jobs` row and starts a Cloudflare Workflow instance; Workflows are included in Workers Free and Paid, allow 30 s of CPU per step on Paid (configurable to 5 min), up to 10,000 steps, and `step.sleep` up to 365 days, which is what lets a go-live job wait days for a client to fix DNS ([Workflows limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workflows/reference/limits.mdx); [Workflows pricing](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workflows/reference/pricing.mdx)). The one hard constraint is that `astro build` cannot run inside a Worker, so the bundle step either fetches a prebuilt template bundle for `template@version` from R2 (build once per template version in CI, since per-client differences are bindings, vars and content in the site's own D1) or triggers a build runner: Workers Builds includes 6,000 build minutes a month on Paid at $0.005 per extra minute ([Workers Builds](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/ci-cd/builds/limits-and-pricing/)), or a GitHub Actions `workflow_dispatch` running `cloudflare/wrangler-action@v3` with `deploy --name site-<slug>` ([rwsdk](https://rwsdk.com/blog/zero-drama-cloudflare-workers-github-actions), secondary). The upload step calls `PUT /accounts/{account_id}/workers/dispatch/namespaces/{ns}/scripts/{script_name}` as multipart with a `metadata` JSON carrying `main_module`, `bindings`, `tags` and `compatibility_date`, with static assets uploaded first via an assets-upload-session ([WfP platform examples](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/reference/platform-examples.mdx)); bindings must be in that metadata, each user Worker sees only what is explicitly attached, so every site gets its own D1, KV and R2 ([WfP bindings](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/bindings.mdx)); tags like `account-<id>` and `demo` or `prod` enable `DELETE .../scripts?tags=account-<id>:yes` for a one-click teardown ([WfP tags](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/tags.mdx)). On Pattern B the equivalent is a plain `wrangler deploy --name site-<slug>` from the runner with a generated `wrangler.jsonc`. The route step writes `hostname:<slug>.preview.yourbrand.com → site-<slug>` to the routing KV; the go-live step POSTs the custom hostname with `ssl.method: "http"` and polls GET until status and `ssl.status` are `active`, with intermediate `ssl.status` values of `initializing`, `pending_validation`, `pending_issuance` and `pending_deployment` ([Custom hostnames create](https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/create), secondary) and hostname states including Pending, Active, Blocked and Moved ([Validation status](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/domain-support/hostname-validation/validation-status.mdx)). Each step writes back to `deploy_jobs.result_json` and `sites`, and the site detail page shows `script_name`, preview URL, custom hostname with status chips, a "Re-check" button, and the last five jobs.

Token and secret design: one account-scoped API token used only by the Workflow Worker, with Workers Scripts, Workers KV Storage, D1 and R2 Write on the account and SSL and Certificates plus DNS Write on the SaaS zone only ([API token permissions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/reference/permissions/)), stored as a Worker secret, never a Global API Key. Per-client Stripe, ShipStation and Mailchimp keys belong in the site Worker's own secret bindings (64 variables on Free, 128 on Paid, 5 KB each ([Workers limits](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workers/platform/limits.mdx))), with only non-secret references in D1; Secrets Store, still in beta with about 100 secrets per account, suits shared platform keys rather than per-client ones ([Secrets Store Workers integration](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/secrets-store/integrations/workers.mdx)). Protect the dashboard in layers: a Cloudflare Access application on `admin.yourbrand.com` with Include = your emails and Require = your IP ranges or Country = US, so the login page itself is invisible to the internet ([Access policies](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-one/access-controls/policies/index.mdx)); better-auth's passkey plugin, powered by SimpleWebAuthn, as the primary factor ([better-auth passkey docs](https://github.com/better-auth/better-auth/blob/main/docs/content/docs/plugins/passkey.mdx)); the admin plugin's role check in middleware for every `/admin/*` route; and an Access service token for the Workflow and cron callers. Cloudflare's own "internal static sites platform" template, which puts every published site behind Access on WfP, is the closest reference implementation for a demo-site publisher ([WfP get started](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/get-started.mdx)).

### Outreach and demo-site legality baked into the pipeline

Cold B2B email is legal under CAN-SPAM's opt-out model, with no B2B exemption: accurate headers, an honest subject line, identification as an ad, a physical postal address, a one-click opt-out honored within 10 business days, and penalties of **up to $53,088 per email** ([EmailListValidation](https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/does-canspam-apply-to-b2b-cold-email-outreach/)). Cold texts or autodialed calls to any mobile number need prior express consent under the TCPA at $500–$1,500 per violation, and a business mobile is treated like a personal one absent a prior relationship ([Wipfli](https://www.wipfli.com/insights/articles/does-tcpa-apply-to-b2b-marketing-key-rules)), so SMS is not an outreach channel for numbers found in FSQ or Google data; the email template should carry your address and an unsubscribe link that flips `do_not_contact`, and every send should run a suppression check. For the demo sites themselves, the defense for using a business's name is nominative fair use, whose three factors are that the service is not readily identifiable without the mark, only as much of the mark as necessary is used, and nothing suggests sponsorship or endorsement ([Playboy v. Welles](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1233126.html)). Translate that into practice: serve demos on `demo-<slug>.yourplatform.com` with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`; gate them behind Access OTP or a signed link; use the business name in plain text only, never the logo or brand photography; use your own placeholder imagery and a generic sample menu or catalog, never pasted Google or Yelp reviews or their site's text, since Google's terms specifically forbid copying reviews ([Google Maps Platform Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)) and photo copyright belongs to whoever took them; show a persistent "Unsolicited concept by <you>; not affiliated with or endorsed by <business>" banner; and delete the Worker and hostname immediately on request, logging the takedown in the CRM.

# usage: bash verify.sh statio shopstead pundak waystead mansio mercantry
for n in "$@"; do

    case $tld in com) u="https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/$n.$tld";; *) u="https://rdap.org/domain/$n.$tld";; esac

  done

  printf "%-14s npm    -> %s\n" "$n" "$(curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' "https://registry.npmjs.org/$n")"    # 404 = free

## Conclusion

The research reframes the venture in two ways. First, the economics invert the agency model: with Cloudflare at a flat $5/month and no per-site fee, your cost of goods is effectively zero and every price point is pure value capture, which means the binding constraint is founder hours and the thing to engineer first is not the platform but the demo generator, the templated, data-driven build that turns a Foursquare row or an Etsy shop name into a watermarked preview URL in minutes rather than hours. A $0-down offer only works if the ninety percent of demos that never convert cost you almost nothing. Second, the handoff friction that most platforms treat as lock-in is, for this audience, the product: a marketplace seller's deepest fear is being hostage to someone else's algorithm and fee schedule, and a local owner's is being hostage to a web guy, so "you own your domain, your Stripe, your customer list and your database export from day one" is both the honest contract and the sharpest pitch against Shopify, Etsy and the nephew.

There is also an asymmetry the segment choice must respect: local businesses are programmatically findable through Apache-licensed data but pay the lower lifetime value per hour of selling, while marketplace sellers are the better product fit for your reference build but can only be found through warm channels because every marketplace API and policy is hostile to lead generation. The sensible sequence is to let outbound demo-first selling to local service businesses fund the first year while the seller storefront, sold through craft fairs, Instagram and your own author network, becomes the repeatable product that justifies the 44–60 days of platform extraction. The name decision is the one thing that should wait on data you do not yet have: run the script, search the marks, and only then commit to Statio, Shopstead or Pundak.
