Research method note: official pricing pages (shopify.com, wix.com, squarespace.com, squareup.com, stripe.com, taxjar.com, shipstation.com, mailchimp.com, help.etsy.com, ebay.com, sell.amazon.com, owner.com, chownow.com, slice.com) were blocked by the session's egress proxy, so every figure below comes from search-engine summaries of third-party pricing trackers, review sites, press releases and SEC filings, dated 2026 unless noted. ## Key question 1: Website builder and e-commerce platform pricing in 2026 (incl. agency/white-label programs)

### Takeaway
A solo seller's cheapest credible "own store" today is $0–$29/mo (Big Cartel free, Square Online Free, Fourthwall free, Ecwid $5, Shopify Starter $5 + 5%) and a serious store is $29–$39/mo plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing (Shopify Basic $39, Wix Core $29, Squarespace Core $36, BigCommerce Core $29, Webflow Ecommerce Standard $29). A local brochure site is $9.99–$25/mo on GoDaddy/Hostinger/Wix Light/Squarespace Basic. White-label agency builders (Duda, Sitejet, Brizy, Simvoly, GoHighLevel) price the agency at $29–$497/mo with $5–$17 per additional site, which anchors the founder's own cost-of-goods comparison at well under $10/site/mo.

### Inferences
- The founder's cost floor is below every white-label builder: Cloudflare Workers Paid is $5/mo per account, not per site ([costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/cloud-infrastructure/cloudflare-pages-workers/)), versus $5–$17/site/mo on Sitejet/Duda/Brizy. That is a cost advantage, not a pricing advantage; customers price against Shopify/Wix/Square, not against Duda.
- "Cheaper than Shopify" is a weak pitch because $0/mo options (Square Online Free, Big Cartel Free, Fourthwall Free) already exist; the founder's differentiation has to be speed, done-for-you setup, and owning Stripe/customer data, not list price.
- Squarespace's 2%-on-Basic and Shopify Starter's 5% show that platforms monetize low-tier sellers through take rates; a 0%-take, flat-fee position is a clear, explainable contrast.

## Key question 3: Agency and freelancer benchmarks for local-business websites in 2026

### Takeaway
A 5–10 page small-business site costs $1,500–$5,000 from a freelancer and $5,000–$15,000 from an agency (Clutch 2025 average $12k–$15k), maintenance/care plans run $50–$500/mo, and the "$0 down, $99–$199/mo with a 12-month minimum" website-as-a-service model is an established, widely advertised offer. Recurring web contracts reportedly churn 20–35% per year.

### Cited Findings
- Freelancers: $1,500–$5,000 for a 5–10 page small-business site; agencies $5,000–$15,000 — [websitesetup](https://websitesetup.org/how-much-does-a-website-cost/); [levitate](https://www.levitate.ai/blog-posts/average-website-design-cost-for-small-businesses-in-2026)
- Clutch 2025 survey: average small business spends $12,000–$15,000 on a new website with a professional team — [merge.rocks](https://merge.rocks/blog/how-much-does-website-design-cost-in-2026-an-honest-pricing-guide)
- WordPress developers $50–$95/hr; 40–80 hours per small-business site = $2,000–$7,600 — [websitesetup](https://websitesetup.org/how-much-does-a-website-cost/)
- Upwork 2026: entry $10–$25/hr, intermediate $25–$60/hr, expert $60–$120+/hr; web designers junior $20–$40, mid $40–$75 (avg ~$58), senior $75–$130, expert $130–$220/hr — [Upwork hourly rates guide](https://www.upwork.com/guides/hourly-rates); [golance](https://golance.com/hiring/best-freelance-web-designers-hourly-rate); [mantlr](https://mantlr.com/blog/freelance-designer-pricing-2026)

- Maintenance/care plans: $25–$600+/mo overall; WebFX 2026 guide $50–$500/mo for a small-business site; full-service plans $100–$200/mo; retainers $75–$2,500/mo by site size — [seahawkmedia](https://seahawkmedia.com/wordpress/wordpress-maintenance-packages/); [apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost); [redefineweb](https://redefineweb.com/blog/website-maintenance-package-pricing/)

### Inferences
- At $99–$149/mo and a 1-page-per-hour Astro template workflow, payback on a $0-down site is immediate if the build takes under ~8 hours (opportunity cost at $50–$95/hr = $400–$760), and a 12-month minimum recovers the build even at 30% annual churn.
- A $0-down WaaS undercuts the freelancer $1,500–$5,000 one-time price by shifting the owner's decision from "spend $3k" to "spend $99/mo", which is the main reason the model persists.

### Gaps
- No primary survey (Clutch/WebFX report itself) was fetchable; figures are as relayed by secondary guides.
- No hard data on average time-per-demo or time-per-build for template-driven agencies; the 40–80 hour figure is for custom WordPress builds.

## Key question 4: Marketplace fees 2026 and the off-ramp math

### Takeaway
Etsy takes roughly 10.6% of a US seller's GMS before Offsite Ads (6.5% transaction + 3% + $0.25 processing + $0.20 listing renewals) and 11.8–13% with mandatory 12% Offsite Ads above $10k/yr; Amazon takes 15% referral in most categories plus $39.99/mo; eBay takes 13.6% + $0.30–$0.40/order without a Store. Moving 25% of a $5,000/mo Etsy seller's volume to a Stripe store saves only about $87–$117/mo net of Stripe fees, so a flat platform fee must stay under roughly $50/mo (or the seller must divert more than 25%) for the fee math alone to work; the stronger argument is owning the buyer list and escaping Offsite Ads and algorithm risk.

### Cited Findings

**Etsy (US sellers)**
- $0.20 listing fee per listing (renews on each sale); 6.5% transaction fee on item price + shipping; US payment processing 3% + $0.25 — [eufymake](https://us.eufymake.com/blogs/business-ideas/how-much-does-etsy-take-per-sale); [crosslist](https://crosslist.com/blog/etsy-review); [edesk](https://www.edesk.com/blog/etsy-seller-fees)

## Key question 6: The spec-work / "build the demo first" sales motion

### Takeaway
Evidence favors personalized, pre-built demos over generic pitches (3–5x reply lift when referencing the prospect's own site; 15% reply rates reported with Loom; one agency claims ~40% higher close with a merchant-named demo), but baseline cold-email conversion to small businesses is brutal (0.37% replies in one 820-email test; 1–5% average), 17–29% of US small businesses still have no site and cost is no longer their main objection, and the design profession (AIGA) formally discourages spec work because of wasted hours, IP ambiguity and reputational risk.

### Cited Findings
- Clutch (Aug 2025, Top Design Firms lineage): 83% of US small businesses have a website (64% in 2018), so 17% are offline; cost as the top objection fell from 26% of hold-outs (2018) to 16% (2025); no-code tools drove adoption — [Clutch via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250821848152/en/Clutch-Report-No-Code-Tools-Fuel-Website-Growth-Yet-17-of-Small-Businesses-are-Still-Offline); [Clutch press release](https://clutch.co/press-releases/survey-shows-opportunity-small-businesses-improve-web-presence)
- Other surveys put adoption at 71–73% (Zippia, Forbes Advisor), i.e. 27–29% without a site; a 91,904-site 2026 census found 17% of local businesses with no site — [review42](https://resources.review42.com/what-percentage-of-small-businesses-have-a-website/); [webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/small-business-statistics); [moneyzine](https://moneyzine.com/startup-resources/small-business-website-statistics/)
- Cold email 2025 benchmarks: average reply 1–5% (best campaigns 8–15%); another dataset: open 27.7%, reply 5.1%; conversion ~1.5% — [folderly](https://generate.folderly.com/blog/cold-email-statistics-2025); [cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-response-rate)
- Indie Hackers test (800+ emails): 820 emails to small businesses produced 3 replies (0.37%); 149 to agencies produced 3 in 48 hours (2.0%); a "free demo" offer converted better than any pricing discussion; referencing specific website issues lifted replies 3–5x over generic praise — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/800-cold-emails-later-heres-what-actually-moves-the-needle-and-what-s-a-complete-waste-of-time-1e67d7e295)
- Loom outreach: one practitioner reports 15% reply rates vs 5–8% for text emails by screen-sharing the prospect's site and pointing out 2–3 issues; one agency sends 100–300 cold Looms/day and books "a handful" of meetings per week — [RevGenius](https://community.revgenius.com/members/4df870b1-30f2-4606-8730-80dafdf9a94f); [swipefile](https://swipefile.com/send-a-3-minute-loom-double-your-close-rate)
- Agency blog claim: a custom demo carrying the merchant's name raises close rate by about 40%; advice to frame redesigns around money/leads/trust and never bash the current site "even if it looks like something their 14-year-old nephew built" — [kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/pitch-selling-website-local-business-2026) (single-agency claim, no sample size)
- "Getting nephewed" / "my son is doing it" is a long-standing objection in web-design communities — [SitePoint thread](https://www.sitepoint.com/community/t/my-son-is-doing-it/55992); [buzzboard objection handling](https://buzzboard.ai/?p=42012)
- Reddit consensus (r/webdev, r/webdesign 2025–26): generic agency outreach is deleted; "make one sample teardown, send a short note: I mocked up the fix here"; a 2026 thread recommends building a free AI draft, walking it through on a Google Meet, and closing on the call; a student-run agency reported acquisition is far harder than building — [Reddit snapshot 1](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tu1wga/snapshots/2026-06-01T19%3A47%3A06.64225Z); [Reddit snapshot 2](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1uhutg0/snapshots/2026-07-02T21%3A43%3A35.23287Z); [r/webdev getting first clients](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/webdev/comments/1j8kghd/getting_my_first_clients)
- Automation of the motion already exists: "LocalBoss"-style agents find businesses with no site, build and deploy a bespoke demo from public photos/reviews/hours, and write the pitch — [site-prospector skill listing](https://claudskills.com/skills/site-prospector/SKILL.md)
- AIGA "strongly discourages" speculative work; risks cited: superficial, strategy-free output; disrespect signal; IP/legal disputes when a rejected comp resembles the later launch; No!Spec provides response templates — [boostlabs](https://boostlabs.com/blog/design-spec-work/); [Zeldman](https://zeldman.com/2007/08/14/dont-design-on-spec/); [inkbotdesign](https://inkbotdesign.com/spec-work-in-design/)

### Inferences
- The addressable "no website" pool is 17–29% of small businesses, but the Clutch data says these hold-outs are not price-blocked (only 16% cite cost), so a $0-down demo mostly removes effort and risk objections, not price objections; the demo must be good enough to make "do nothing" feel costly.
- Realistic funnel for a solo founder (inference from the benchmarks above): 100 personalized demo emails → 5–15 replies → 2–5 calls → 1–2 closes; at 1–3 hours per templated Astro demo that is 100–300 hours per 1–2 clients unless demos are generated semi-automatically from Google Business Profile data. Build-time automation is therefore the make-or-break of this motion, not sales skill.
- Unsolicited demos carry three risks worth contracting around: (a) wasted builds (expect >90% waste), (b) the owner reusing the demo's copy/photos with a nephew, (c) brand/IP complaints if the demo uses the business's logo or photos without permission; mitigate with watermarked preview URLs, robots noindex, and a 7–14 day auto-expiry.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of demo-first vs pitch-first close rates for local-business web sales was found; all figures are practitioner reports.
- No source quantified average time-per-demo for template-driven agencies.

## Key question 7: Direction analysis — productized service (A) vs managed multi-tenant platform (B) vs self-serve SaaS (C)

### Takeaway
Public comps show that pure software subscriptions earn ~80% gross margins (Wix Creative Subscriptions) while payments-heavy models earn ~33–48% (Wix Business Solutions, Shopify blended), that self-serve "cheaper Shopify" entrants have repeatedly died or shrunk (Volusion −74% stores since 2020, Tictail and Selz acquired and shut), and that the wedges that worked were vertical and sales-led (Owner.com, $100M+ ARR at $249–$499/mo) or creator-niche with $0 entry (Fourthwall, Big Cartel, Gumroad). For a solo founder, A is fastest to revenue and should feed B; C is the slowest and least defensible.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify Q2 2026: gross margin 47.7%; merchant solutions revenue +37% to $2.78B vs subscription solutions +22% to $802M; payments gross margin is lower than subscriptions, dragging blended margin down — [Shopify 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001594805/000159480526000047/shop-20260630.htm); [Quartr Q2 2026 summary](https://quartr.com/events/shopify-inc-shop-q2-2026_3eya9uq8)

- Wix Q2 2026: non-GAAP gross margin 67% total; Creative Subscriptions 80%; Business Solutions 33%; Creative Subscriptions revenue +15% to $398.4M; ARR $1.96B (+15%); total bookings $569M — [StreetInsider Wix Q2 2026](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Wix+Reports+Second+Quarter+2026+Results/26859973.html); [Seeking Alpha Wix Q1 2026](https://seekingalpha.com/pr/20512579)

**Comparison (opinion, grounded in the findings above)**

| Dimension | A: Productized service | B: Managed multi-tenant platform | C: Self-serve SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first revenue | Days (first close) | Weeks (after 3–10 A clients prove the template) | Months (onboarding, billing, docs, support, marketing) |

| Support burden | Per-client, manual, but paid for | Shared codebase; one fix propagates | Highest per dollar; self-serve users expect chat/docs |
| Churn | 20–35%/yr (agency) | Lower if hosting + domain + email are bundled (switching cost) | 3–4%/mo typical SaaS; higher for $9–$29 tiers |
| Defensibility | Relationships + local reputation | Data (orders, lists) + integrations + handoff friction | Weak vs Shopify/Wix/Square at $0 |
| Fundability | Not venture-fundable; cash-flow business | Fundable only with a vertical wedge (Owner.com pattern) | Historically poor (Volusion, Tictail, Selz, Storenvy) |
| Fit with solo Astro + Cloudflare + Stripe + ShipStation + Mailchimp | Excellent today | Good once templates and a tenant config layer exist | Poor without a team |

### Inferences
- Wix's 80% subscription margin and Shopify's 47.7% blended margin point the same way: keep the founder's revenue in software/service subscriptions and leave payments (and their fraud/chargeback exposure) with Stripe on the client's own account.
- The graveyard is self-serve horizontal (Volusion, Tictail, Selz, Storenvy); the winners are vertical + sales-led (Owner.com) or niche + free-entry (Fourthwall, Big Cartel). A solo founder cannot fund a free-entry land-grab, so the realistic path is A → B within one or two niches.
- GoHighLevel's $497/mo SaaS Mode and Duda's $149 white-label tier show that agencies pay $150–$500/mo for the privilege of operating a multi-tenant platform; the founder already owns that layer, which is the structural reason B is attractive once A validates demand.

### Gaps
- Neither Shopify nor Wix discloses merchant-level churn; the retention figures above are scale-dependent and secondary.
- No reliable post-mortems with numbers were found for Zibbet or Storenvy.

## Key question 8: Recommended starting segment and suggested pricing/packaging

### Takeaway
Opinion: start with (1) Etsy/handmade and small eBay sellers who already ship physical goods, because the founder's reference build (catholicjoesuperhero.com: Astro + Stripe Checkout + ShipStation + Mailchimp) is exactly that product and the fee math and 5.6M-seller pool are quantifiable; run (2) local service businesses (barbers, landscapers, trades) as a second lane with a $0-down, $99–$149/mo site-as-a-service; defer restaurants/online ordering because the need is order routing and POS, where Owner.com, ChowNow, Slice, Square and DoorDash Storefront already compete at $0–$499/mo. Price seller storefronts at $29–$49/mo (0% take) with optional $199–$499 migration, and local sites at $99–$199/mo with $0–$499 setup; gross margin before labor exceeds 90% because Cloudflare is $5/mo total.

### Inferences (suggested packaging; opinion)

**Seller storefront (Etsy/eBay/Amazon off-ramp)** — positioned as "0% take rate, your Stripe, your list"

| Tier | Price | Includes | Pass-through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | $29/mo (or $290/yr) | Up to 100 products, Stripe Checkout, Stripe Tax wired, discount codes, order export to Pirate Ship/ShipStation, Brevo/Mailchimp signup form, custom domain, SSL, CDN | Stripe 2.9% + $0.30; Stripe Tax 0.5% on registered-state sales; postage |

| Migration (one-time) | $199–$499 | Done-for-you import of listings/photos, domain, email templates, first campaign | — |

Rationale: at 25% diversion of a $5k/mo shop the seller nets $87–$117/mo in avoided fees; $29–$49 leaves them visibly ahead and sits below Shopify Basic ($39) + apps ($30–$80). A 1% take-rate variant ($9/mo + 1%) yields only ~$12.50/mo on $1,250 diverted and muddies the "0% take" message, so a flat fee is preferable.

**Local business site-as-a-service** — positioned as "live in 7 days, $0 down"

| Tier | Price | Includes | Pass-through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presence | $99/mo, $0 setup, 12-month minimum | 5-page Astro site, hosting/SSL/CDN, domain at cost, Google Business Profile sync, contact/quote forms, 1 content change/mo | Domain renewal (~$10–$15/yr) |
| Bookings/Leads | $149/mo | + booking widget (e.g., Square Appointments/Cal embed), reviews feed, SMS/email lead alerts, 2 changes/mo | Any SMS cost |
| Growth | $199–$249/mo | + monthly content/SEO page, quarterly report, priority support | — |
| Optional setup | $0–$499 | $0 for demo-first closes; $499 when the owner wants custom photography/copy | — |

Rationale: market WaaS is $99–$125/mo; agency one-time builds are $1.5k–$15k; care plans $50–$500/mo. With Cloudflare at $5/mo and no per-site fee, contribution margin is >90% before labor; the binding constraint is founder hours (build 3–8 hrs, support 0.5–1 hr/mo), so ~50 sites at $120 average is ~$6k MRR for roughly 25–50 support hours/month.

**What to bundle vs pass through**: bundle hosting, SSL, CDN, uptime, security updates, domain (at cost), transactional email (Resend free tier), and Stripe Tax wiring; pass through Stripe processing, Stripe Tax's 0.5%, postage, ShipStation ($14.99–$29.99+) and paid email tiers (Mailchimp Essentials $13+, Klaviyo $20+).

## Key question 9: Legal and business-structure notes (merchant of record, Stripe Connect, sales tax on SaaS/web services, hosting contract terms)

### Takeaway
Keep each client as merchant of record on their own Stripe account, connected via Stripe Connect Standard (free) so the founder keeps dashboard visibility and can optionally take an application fee; Express/Custom cost $2/active account/mo + 0.25% + $0.25 per payout and Custom makes the platform the MoR. If the founder is in Georgia, SaaS and most services are not subject to sales tax, but "specified digital products" have been taxable since Jan 1, 2024 (state 4%, combined 6–9%); standard agency subscription contracts use a license-not-ownership model with 30-day notice after a minimum term, buy-out rights, and client ownership of domain and content.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe Connect: Standard free; Express and Custom $2 per active account per month + 0.25% + $0.25 per payout; Express processing fees equal Standard; with Express the connected account is merchant of record, with Custom the platform is MoR and must build onboarding and handle compliance — [paymentcloudinc](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/stripe-connect/); [dev.to multi-tenant Connect 2026](https://dev.to/astraedus/building-a-multi-tenant-saas-with-stripe-connect-in-2026-jjn)
- Georgia: does not tax SaaS or custom software; most services non-taxable; specified digital products (downloads with permanent right of use) taxable since Jan 1, 2024, streaming/subscription access not taxable; state rate 4%, combined ~6–9%; economic nexus $100,000 or 200 transactions — [numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/saas-sales-tax-georgia); [taxcloud](https://taxcloud.com/sales-tax/georgia/); [kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/georgia); [afternoon](https://www.afternoon.co/blog/georgia-sales-tax-guide)
- Subscription web contracts: IP often remains the agency's with a non-exclusive, revocable license for the subscription term; some contracts transfer content ownership to the client unless terminated before the minimum term; termination on 30 days' notice after minimum term; early-termination fees; site may go offline at termination; buy-out at agency discretion; some agencies state domains and content belong to the client and leave with them; hosting, maintenance and standard support included in the monthly fee — [Magellan Design T&Cs](https://magellandesign.com/standard-terms-and-conditions-of-business); [Custody Design sample contract](https://custodydesign.com/sample-contract); [Business-in-a-Box template](https://www.business-in-a-box.com/template/website-design-hosting-and-commercial-services-agreement-D824); [ProposalKit hosting agreement](https://www.proposalkit.com/htm/legal-contract-templates/web-hosting-contracts/short-web-site-hosting-agreement.htm)
- Stripe Tax is charged only in jurisdictions where the merchant has an active registration — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/understanding-stripe-tax-pricing)

### Inferences
- Client-owned Stripe + Connect Standard means the founder never touches funds, never becomes MoR for the client's sales tax, chargebacks or 1099-K, and still sees orders; the trade-off is that Standard accounts can disconnect the platform at will, which is acceptable for a service business and even a selling point ("you own your Stripe").
- Because SaaS and web services are generally untaxed in Georgia (founder's state unverified), the founder's own invoices likely carry no sales tax there, but clients in other states can trigger tax on SaaS (many states tax SaaS); keep subscription revenue below other states' nexus thresholds or use Stripe Tax on the founder's own Stripe Billing (0.7% of billing volume) once multi-state.
- Recommended handoff terms (opinion): client owns domain, content, Stripe account, customer list and product data from day one; founder owns template code and grants a license; on exit provide a static export of the site and CSV of orders/customers within 30 days; 30-day notice after a 6–12 month minimum; no buy-out needed because the client already owns everything that matters.

### Gaps
- Founder's state was not given; Georgia was used as the illustrative case per the brief. Other states' SaaS taxability was not researched here.
- No source on Stripe's own policy for platforms taking application fees from Standard connected accounts was retrieved; confirm in Stripe Connect docs before relying on it.
