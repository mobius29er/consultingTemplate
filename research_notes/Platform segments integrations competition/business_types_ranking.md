# US small local business types: inventory and ranking for a remote-first, done-for-you website + integrations platform

Scope: US, 2025-2026 data where it exists. Sandbox note: census.gov, api.census.gov, bls.gov, sba.gov, statista, IBISWorld, Clutch, GoDaddy and most vendor pages were blocked by the egress proxy, so every figure below comes from WebSearch result summaries of those sources (25-search budget, fully used) or from the sibling notes in `research_notes/Local storefront platform strategy/`. Where a figure's exact page could not be opened it is marked "(via search summary)". Anything I estimated is marked "(estimate)" with its basis. Official CBP/SUSB establishment counts per 6-digit NAICS could not be pulled and are listed as a gap; the counts below are the best public proxies (BLS QCEW, NACS, NAHB, USDA, IBISWorld "number of businesses", trade-association counts). IBISWorld counts include nonemployer/self-employed operators, so they run far above CBP employer-establishment counts (e.g. 1.06M "hair salons" vs roughly 80-90K employer salons), which matters because the "no website" pool is concentrated in exactly those nonemployer operators.

Related sibling notes (not repeated here): competitor pricing per segment and the "restaurants are the hardest first segment" argument are in `Local storefront platform strategy/business_model_and_market.md`; how to find no-website businesses (Google Places `websiteUri`, Foursquare OS Places, Overture, Apify/Outscraper) is in `Local storefront platform strategy/admin_dashboard_and_leads.md`.

## Key question 1: Which 10 types should the founder pursue first for the primary "no website" segment?

### Takeaway
Scored on the six stated criteria, the first-wave primary segment is home-and-property services (plumbing/HVAC/electrical, landscaping, remodelers/roofers), independent auto repair, and barbers/salons, followed by cleaning services, cafes/bakeries, pizza shops and independent restaurants, liquor/convenience stores and solo lawyers/accountants. The common thread: large pools of single-location, owner-run businesses where 40-57% have no site (home services) or where the only online presence is Instagram/Facebook (salons, food trucks, tattoo), where the engine's presence/services variant is a complete product on day one, and where the owner answers the shop phone.

### Inferences
- The founder's strongest first wave is "trades and property services" plus independent auto repair: the only type-level no-website surveys that exist (Housecall Pro >40%, Jobber >50%, Home Service Base ~57% for cleaners) all point there, the pool is >1.2M businesses, the presence/services engine variant is a complete product, and the owners already pay $100-$300/mo to Housecall Pro/Jobber-type tools, which means they understand paying monthly for leads.
- Barbers/salons and food trucks/tattoo have the highest no-site share but the lowest pay capacity; they are the best brand-building, walk-in, nearby customers at the $500 tier and the worst customers for a $99+/mo recurring plan. Use them for local proof and referrals, not for revenue.
- Restaurants are the largest pool and the founder's named use case, but the Technomic data shows the pool is shrinking 2.3%/yr and the TouchBistro data shows the gap is "direct ordering" (39%) rather than "any website". A static presence site with menu plus a Square/DoorDash Storefront link is sellable; order routing is not something to build first (see sibling note for the $0-$499/mo incumbents).
- Auto parts retail scores lower only because the pool is small and the custom-inventory build is heavy; it is still the right $40K-$100K anchor project because the founder's family store removes the discovery and trust cost.
- Liquor/convenience stores have a very high no-site share and the owner is always present, but they rarely pay for marketing; sell them a $500 presence site, not a subscription.
- IBISWorld counts overstate the "address + phone" pool: a large share of its 1.06M hair salons and 692K landscapers are solo operators with a cell phone and no storefront. The addressable "has address + phone, no site" subset is closer to the employer-establishment count (gap: CBP figures not retrievable here) multiplied by the 17-57% no-site shares.

## Key question 2: Which 5 types are the best secondary "has a website, wants to switch or add integrations" segment and why?

### Takeaway
The secondary segment should be chosen by integration pull rather than by type size: (1) local retailers and makers selling physical goods who need Stripe + ShipStation + Mailchimp (the founder's already-built stack), (2) independent restaurants paying $249-$499/mo to ordering platforms who want direct ordering and email, (3) home-service firms on free Housecall Pro/Jobber sites who need a better front end wired to their job software, (4) professional practices (dental, chiro, law, accounting) on dated agency sites who need booking/intake, and (5) independent auto parts and hardware retailers with legacy inventory systems who are the custom-build ($40K-$100K) buyers.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify commanded 70% of e-commerce platform migrations in 2024, and 2025 migrations are driven by scalability (60% of businesses on outdated platforms) and integration depth (Shopify's 8,000+ apps; integrations drive ~30% of migrations among mid-sized retailers) — [Cart2Cart](https://www.shopping-cart-migration.com/must-know-tips/cart2cart-migration-facts-statistics), [The Commerce Shop](https://www.thecommerceshop.com/blog/ecommerce-platform-migration-process-detailed-guidelines), [Folio3](https://ecommerce.folio3.com/blog/squarespace-to-shopify-migration) (vendor sources; figures unverified)
- Common 2026 reasons to leave Wix/Squarespace: limited SEO/technical control and rising monthly cost once add-ons and e-commerce features are stacked — [O'Brien Media](https://obrienmedia.co.uk/?p=210029) (vendor source)
- Home-service owners with websites are "often unhappy with their site's appearance, functionality and/or cost" and confused about where to turn for help (Housecall Pro) — [PM Magazine](https://pmmag.com/articles/102211-housecall-pro-helps-home-service-companies-build-free-professional-websites); Housecall Pro and Jobber now bundle free website builders, so the installed base of "free but basic" home-service sites is growing — [PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/9644-housecall-pro-offers-website-buider), [HVAC Insider](https://hvacinsider.com/jobber-empowers-home-service-pros-to-attract-more-customers-and-grow-smarter-with-new-digital-marketing-tools/)
- Restaurant ordering incumbents charge $0/mo (Square Online Free, DoorDash Storefront) to $119-$499/mo plus 2.9-3.5% processing and per-order fees (ChowNow, Owner.com, Toast, Popmenu; Slice 5-7% of sales) — sibling note `business_model_and_market.md` (section "Restaurants")
- 61% of verified Google Business Profiles include a direct website link; ~39% lack one or have not linked it — [WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/) (secondary roundup)
- Only 26% of dental practices offer online scheduling; 94% of patients more likely to choose a provider with it — [Clerri](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-practice-scheduling-statistics/)
- Among small-to-medium nonprofits, 70% have no email marketing strategy and over 50% use no CRM or marketing automation — [Firespring](https://firespring.com/solutions-for-nonprofits/the-most-common-problem-that-nonprofits-face-today)
- Etsy/Amazon sellers already pay 10.6-13% effective Etsy fees or 15% Amazon referral + $39.99/mo, and 21% of Amazon sellers were on Shopify with 35% planning to add it (2023) — sibling note `business_model_and_market.md` (sections "Marketplace fees" and "Recommendation")

### Inferences
- Secondary type 1, physical-goods retailers and makers (boutiques, gift shops, Etsy/Amazon sellers, farm stands): the founder's CatholicJoe stack (Astro + Stripe Checkout + ShipStation + Mailchimp) is literally the product; the integration menu for them is Stripe, PayPal, ShipStation, Mailchimp, QuickBooks, Google Merchant Center, Meta catalog, Etsy/Shopify import. Highest fit, lowest build risk.
- Secondary type 2, restaurants on paid ordering platforms: the $249-$499/mo price umbrella funds a $99-$199/mo site that owns the menu, email list (Mailchimp) and reservation/ordering links, with the DoorDash/Toast/Square ordering integration as the first "food" app in the menu. The integration is the sale; the website is the wrapper.
- Secondary type 3, home-service firms on Housecall Pro/Jobber/ServiceTitan free sites: they need a faster, better-looking front end whose quote and booking forms post into their job software via webhooks/Zapier-style connectors; the owner never sees an API key if the connector handles it. This is also the largest primary-segment overlap, so one engine variant serves both lanes.
- Secondary type 4, professional practices with dated agency sites: willingness to pay is highest, the need is booking/intake (dental 26% online booking), but every integration (NexHealth, Dentrix, Clio Grow, Calendly) and HIPAA handling adds support burden; treat as a $2K-$10K project tier, not a $99/mo tier.
- Secondary type 5, independent auto parts and hardware retailers: the need is inventory sync (point-of-sale and parts catalogs) and a searchable online catalog; this is the custom $40K-$100K lane and should be sold one at a time through the founder's family store as reference.
- Ranking rationale: types 1 and 3 reuse what exists; type 2 has the biggest price umbrella; types 4 and 5 have the biggest ticket but the longest build.

### Gaps
- No survey of small businesses by type that asks "do you plan to switch website platforms" was found; platform-switching data is from migration vendors about e-commerce stores only.
- Share of restaurants currently paying Owner.com/ChowNow/Popmenu-class fees (the real secondary pool size) was not found.
- Share of home-service firms using Housecall Pro/Jobber free sites was not found.

## Key question 3: What evidence exists that "has phone and address but no website" correlates with specific types, business age or owner age?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin and mostly about size and owner age rather than type: the smallest firms (one-third of the smallest businesses have no site), older owners (a third of owners in their 50s-70s rate "getting online" a top challenge, and 51% of owners are over 55), and home-service trades (>40-57% no site) are the documented correlates; the type correlation for restaurants is real but from a circa-2013 study, and Google Business Profile data shows 14% (strict) to 39% (loose) of listed businesses have no linked website.

### Cited Findings
- One-third of the smallest businesses have no website and half do not use AI (Small Business Majority, 2025) — [Small Business Majority](https://smallbusinessmajority.org/node/389880)
- 2025 Clutch: 17% offline; top reason now "not relevant to my industry", cost only 16% — [Clutch via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250821848152/en/Clutch-Report-No-Code-Tools-Fuel-Website-Growth-Yet-17-of-Small-Businesses-are-Still-Offline)
- 2018 Clutch: 36% had no website; 21% used social media instead of a website; 46% of those relied most on Facebook; 26% cited cost — [Agility PR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-news/marketing-news/despite-risks-small-businesses-use-social-media-instead-websites/), [Clutch press release](https://clutch.co/press-releases/small-businesses-use-social-media-instead-website-despite-risks)
- Top Design Firms 2022 (n=1,003): 27% had no website; 87% of those planned to invest in one — [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/sales-marketing/one-third-small-businesses-no-website-survey-finds/), [MarTech Cube](https://www.martechcube.com/?p=117375)
- BIA/Kelsey (older data): 54% of SMBs had a Facebook page, 60% a website — [Marketing Charts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/digital-38055)
- Older microbusiness owners are less comfortable getting online: 37% of owners in their 60s, 36% of those 70+, and 30% of those in their 50s named getting online a top challenge (AARP/GoDaddy microbusiness research) — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/work/small-business/starting-microbusiness/)
- 51% of US small businesses are owned by people older than 55 (SCORE survey of 3,000+ owners) — [Gene Marks](https://www.genemarks.com/columns/most-small-business-owners-are-middle-aged-not-cool-kids-the-media-loves)
- Consumers 55+ are much less concerned about whether a local business has a website or how it looks (BrightLocal 2016) — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/2016/03/04/gender-age-what-consumers-want-from-local-business-websites/)
- Google Business Profile: 15.3% of 46,662 sampled profiles returned no website via the Places API (14.1% US, 17.4% Netherlands); Commerce City CO 31.5%, Aurora CO 16.6%, Wheat Ridge CO 16.4% (2026 study) — [Recensio reviews benchmark](https://recensio-ai.lovable.app/reviews-benchmark-report) (small vendor study, methodology not fetchable); ~61% of verified profiles include a website link — [WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/)
- A 91,904-site 2026 census found 17% of local businesses with no site — sibling note citing [Moneyzine](https://moneyzine.com/startup-resources/small-business-website-statistics/)
- Home services: >40% no site (Housecall Pro) — [PM Magazine](https://pmmag.com/articles/102211-housecall-pro-helps-home-service-companies-build-free-professional-websites); cleaners: 124,203 companies vs 53,227 websites — [Home Service Base](https://homeservicebase.com/reports/house-cleaning-us)
- Restaurants: <50% of independents had a site vs almost all chains (circa 2013) — [SmartBrief](https://www.smartbrief.com/original/closing-your-virtual-front-door-why-do-half-americas-independent)
- Auto repair: 3 in 4 shops are single-location owner-operators — [Orbital](https://www.withorbital.com/data/how-many-auto-repair-shops-in-the-us/)
- Churches: 78% of Protestant churches have a site, but attendance size is the driver (substantially all >500 attendance, just over half <50 attendance) — [Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/many-churches-use-website-like-yellow-page-ad-study-finds.html)
- Farms: internet access 75% (2017) to 79% (2022), so about 1 in 5 farms has no internet at all — [Seed World](https://www.seedworld.com/us/2024/02/15/usda-releases-2022-census-of-agriculture-data/)
- Empty Google `websiteUri` is a first-pass signal only; Facebook pages, Linktree, Square/Toast ordering pages and marketplace shops often appear as "no website" or as a social URL — sibling note `admin_dashboard_and_leads.md`

### Inferences
- The documented correlates line up as: (a) fewer employees, (b) older owner, (c) trades and personal services where work comes by word of mouth, (d) single-location independents vs chains. "Not relevant to my industry" as the 2025 top reason is itself type evidence: the remaining hold-outs believe their trade does not need a site, which fits home services, auto repair, liquor/convenience and cash-based personal services.
- Practical discovery filter for the founder's dashboard: Google Places `websiteUri` empty OR pointing to facebook.com/instagram.com/linktr.ee/square.site/toasttab.com, AND a phone number AND a street address (not a service-area-only listing), AND rating count above ~10 (an operating business with customers). Expect roughly 14-20% of listings to pass the strict "no website" test in a typical US city and more in older, lower-income or rural trade areas (Commerce City CO at 31.5% is a hint).
- Because the gap is driven by owner age, the sales motion must be phone and in-person, with the website demo already built; an email or self-serve funnel will mostly reach the 83% who already have a site.

### Gaps
- No study was found that cross-tabulates "no website" by NAICS type with a 2024-2026 sample; the restaurant figure is ~13 years old and the GBP study is a small vendor sample.
- No study cross-tabulating no-website status by business age (years operating) was found.
- GoDaddy Venture Forward's own microbusiness tables (owner age vs domain ownership) could not be fetched; the AARP figures are the best proxy.

## Key question 4: What does each top type need on day one (pages, integrations, content the founder must collect)?

### Takeaway
Every top-10 type can launch on five to seven pages (home, services/menu, about, reviews, contact/quote, hours/map, gallery) plus Google Business Profile sync, a Stripe-backed deposit or payment link, and Mailchimp; the type-specific day-one integration is the one the owner already uses to run the business (Housecall Pro/Jobber for trades, Square/Toast/DoorDash for food, Booksy/Square Appointments for salons, NexHealth-class booking for practices), and the founder's biggest content cost is photos and a services/price list, which the owner rarely has.

### Cited Findings
- Consumers expect menus and mobile: only 40% of independent restaurant sites showed a menu and 95% lacked a mobile site in the 2013 study — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=542786); 67% of consumers prefer ordering from the restaurant's own site/app — [Restolabs](https://restolabs.com/blog/online-ordering-statistics-every-restaurateur-should-know)
- 39% of independent FSRs offer direct online ordering; 99% use at least one platform — [TouchBistro press release](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/touchbistros-2025-state-of-restaurants-report-reveals-89-per-cent-of-u-s-independent-operators-feel-positive-about-the-use-of-ai)
- Younger clients choose home-service providers by online search and reviews (Jobber) — [HVAC Insider](https://hvacinsider.com/jobber-empowers-home-service-pros-to-attract-more-customers-and-grow-smarter-with-new-digital-marketing-tools/); Housecall Pro and Jobber ship free website builders that connect to their booking/quote tools — [PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/9644-housecall-pro-offers-website-buider)
- 82% of dental patients book from a smartphone; 71% search online before choosing a dentist; 94% prefer providers with online booking — [Clerri](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-practice-scheduling-statistics/), [Sagapixel](https://sagapixel.com/?p=22330)
- Churches use sites mostly as a "Yellow Pages ad"; fewer than half use them for prayer requests (43%), event registration (39%) or process automation (30%) — [Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/many-churches-use-website-like-yellow-page-ad-study-finds.html)
- Google's Places API returns at most 5 reviews per place and prohibits scraping/republishing reviews outside the API; review markup must be for reviews produced on your own site — sibling note `admin_dashboard_and_leads.md` (reviews section)
- Stripe US pricing 2.9% + $0.30 online, Stripe Tax 0.5%; Mailchimp free tier 250 contacts / 500 sends (cut Jan 2026), Essentials from $13/mo — sibling note `business_model_and_market.md` (integration pricing table)

### Inferences (day-one checklist per top type)
- Plumbing/HVAC/electrical: pages = home with emergency phone CTA, services (one page per core service for local SEO), service area map, reviews, financing, contact/quote. Integrations = Google Business Profile, Housecall Pro or Jobber booking/quote webhook (fallback: email + SMS to owner), Stripe for deposits, Mailchimp for seasonal tune-up campaigns, call tracking number optional. Collect = license numbers, insurance proof, 10-20 job photos, price ranges, service list, truck/team photo, 5 customer quotes.
- Landscaping/lawn care: pages = home, services (mowing, hardscape, cleanup), gallery, service area, quote form, reviews. Integrations = GBP, Jobber/Yardbook quote webhook, Stripe recurring for maintenance plans, Mailchimp spring/fall campaigns. Collect = before/after photos (the owner has them on a phone), seasonal price sheet, service zip list.
- Auto repair: pages = home, services, makes serviced, warranty/financing, reviews, appointment request, hours/map. Integrations = GBP, shop-management appointment request (Shopmonkey/Tekmetric/AutoLeap via email/webhook; direct API access varies and is unverified here), Stripe deposit link, Mailchimp service reminders, CarFax/RepairPal badges. Collect = ASE/certifications, shop photos, labor rate, warranty terms, list of services.
- Remodelers/contractors/roofers: pages = home, services, project gallery with case studies, process, financing, license/insurance, quote form. Integrations = GBP, CRM/estimating webhook (JobNimbus, AccuLynx, Buildertrend; unverified API availability), Stripe deposit, Mailchimp. Collect = 20-40 project photos, license numbers, 3-5 project write-ups, financing partner.
- Barbers/salons/nail/tattoo: pages = home, services + prices, team/artists with Instagram feed, gallery, booking CTA, policies (deposits, cancellations). Integrations = Booksy / Square Appointments / Vagaro booking embed or link, Instagram feed, Stripe deposit for tattoo consults, GBP. Collect = price list, team photos, Instagram handle, booking tool login (OAuth to Square where possible).
- Cleaning services: pages = home, services (recurring/deep/move-out), pricing or instant quote, service area, reviews, booking. Integrations = booking/quote form to Housecall Pro/Jobber/ZenMaid webhook, Stripe recurring, Mailchimp. Collect = price matrix by home size, service checklist, insurance/bonding proof.
- Cafes/bakeries: pages = home, menu (structured, mobile-first), order-ahead link, catering/custom-order form, hours/map, events. Integrations = Square Online or Toast ordering link, Stripe deposit for custom orders, Mailchimp/loyalty, Instagram feed, GBP menu sync. Collect = full menu with prices (often only on a chalkboard), 15-30 food photos, allergen notes, seasonal items.
- Pizza shops/independent restaurants: pages = home, menu, order/reserve CTAs, specials, catering, hours/map, reviews. Integrations = DoorDash Storefront / Slice / Toast / Square ordering link as the food variant's first app, OpenTable/Resy link, Mailchimp, GBP. Collect = menu with modifiers and prices, photos, delivery zone, platform logins. Do not promise order routing or POS sync on day one (sibling note).
- Liquor/convenience: pages = home, hours/map, departments, weekly specials, services (lottery, EBT, ATM, delivery partners). Integrations = GBP, Instagram/Facebook feed, DoorDash/Instacart/Drizly-type links where legal. Collect = storefront photos, hours, services list; keep it a $500 presence site.
- Solo lawyers/accountants/tax preparers: pages = home, practice areas/services, about/credentials, consultation booking, intake form, reviews (subject to bar rules), FAQ. Integrations = Calendly/Clio Grow/Lawmatics or TaxDome intake (unverified API/OAuth here), Stripe consult fee, Mailchimp. Collect = bar/CPA numbers, headshots, practice-area copy, disclaimer language.
- Cross-cutting integration menu by lane: Presence/services lane = GBP, Mailchimp, Stripe payment links, booking tool, CRM webhook. Food lane = ordering platform link, reservations, Mailchimp, GBP menu. Retail lane = Stripe/PayPal checkout, ShipStation, Mailchimp, QuickBooks, Google Merchant Center, Meta catalog. Custom inventory lane = POS/inventory sync, Stripe, ShipStation, QuickBooks. OAuth is known to exist for Stripe Connect, Square, Shopify, Mailchimp, QuickBooks, Google and Meta from general knowledge; ShipStation, DoorDash and Toast require API keys or partner programs (unverified in this note; the integrations researcher should confirm).

### Gaps
- No per-type survey of "which pages or features owners asked for" was found; the checklists above are inferred from the needs evidence and the founder's stack.
- Direct API/OAuth availability for shop-management (Shopmonkey, Tekmetric), contractor CRMs (JobNimbus, AccuLynx), salon booking (Booksy, Vagaro) and legal intake tools was not researched here; it belongs to the integrations note.
- Content-collection effort per type (hours) is a judgment with no published benchmark.
