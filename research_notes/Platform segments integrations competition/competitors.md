# Competitive landscape: done-for-you website + integrations + managed platform for US small businesses (2026)

Scope: who the founder competes with for each customer type, from a $500 presence site to a $40k-$100k custom inventory build, with 2026 pricing, positioning, traction and the differentiation thesis. Written October 2026, US market.

Method and provenance note: every vendor site tried this session was blocked by the egress proxy (sec.gov incl. efts and www, businesswire, techcrunch, pulse2, trustpilot, webtonic, getsauce, durable.com, hostinger.com, betakit, last10k, boardroomalpha). All findings below therefore come from WebSearch result extracts of the cited pages (25-search budget fully used); each is tagged "(via search summary)" implicitly. Items marked "(unverified)" come from my own prior knowledge with the last figure and date I know and must be checked on the vendor page before being quoted to a prospect. Where sibling notes already hold a figure, I cite the sibling rather than repeat the research: `Local storefront platform strategy/business_model_and_market.md` (DIY builder and restaurant-ordering price tables, agency benchmarks, direction analysis), `business_types_ranking.md` (segment ranking), `integrations_payments_commerce_accounting.md` and `integrations_marketing_comms_booking.md` (OAuth/API gating per vendor).

## Key question 1: How big is the US SMB web-services and small-business software market, and how many targets have no website?

### Takeaway
The pool is large but every source disagrees on the no-website share (17% to 36%, 2026), and the best-documented spend figure for a 1-10 employee business is a median of only ~$124/month on all software, which caps what a presence-site customer will pay and argues for $99-$199/mo pricing with integrations as the upsell.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 census of 91,904 local-business sites found 17% of local businesses have no website; hair salons have the lowest adoption at 62.6% and SaaS companies the highest at 97% — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/small-business-statistics)
- Other 2026 aggregates: 73%-83% of US small businesses have a website (17%-27% without) — [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/what-percentage-of-small-businesses-have-a-website/); 36% do not have a website — [Moneyzine](https://moneyzine.com/startup-resources/small-business-website-statistics/); 28% do not, 72% do (up from 64% in 2020) — [HandleGrab](https://www.handlegrab.com/blog/small-business-online-presence-statistics)
- Businesses with 1-10 employees spend a median of $124/month on software; 11-50 employees $412/month; the average 10-person SMB spends $847/month on subscriptions (+137% since 2022); the $19-$49/month price point captures 62% of the SMB tool market; 78% of SMBs use integrated platforms rather than standalone tools — [Mewayz SMB Software Spend Report 2026](https://mewayz.com/en/blog/smb-software-spend-report-2026-how-much-small-businesses-actually-pay) and [State of Small Business Software 2026](https://mewayz.com/blog/the-state-of-small-business-software-in-2026-annual-report) (vendor-published report; sample claimed 138,000+ businesses)
- The global AI website builder market is projected at $3.24B in 2026, up from $2.69B in 2025 — [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/ai-website-builder)
- Scale of incumbents the owner already knows: Wix FY2025 revenue $1.99B (+13%), Creative Subscriptions $1.41B, Business Solutions $583.3M — [Wix Q4/FY2025 results](https://seekingalpha.com/pr/20423147), [Wix 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1576789/000162828026015222/wix-20251231.htm); GoDaddy ended 2025 with 20.4 million paying customers and >85% retention — [GoDaddy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000010/gddy-20251231.htm); Squarespace trailing revenue about $1.14B, private since the Permira take-private in October 2024 — [Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/squarespace-statistics/) (aggregator)
- Thryv, the largest listed "done-for-you" SMB reseller, reports 100,000 SaaS clients at $373 monthly ARPU (Q4 2025) and $461.0M SaaS revenue for 2025 — [MarketChameleon summary of Thryv FY2025](https://marketchameleon.com/articles/b/2026/2/27/thryv-saas-growth-ai-platform-2025-results), [Thryv press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260226660000/en)

### Inferences
- The 17% (site-census) and 28%-36% (survey) figures are not contradictory: the census counts businesses visible enough to be crawled, surveys include micro and home-based firms. The sibling discovery note's 14%-20% strict "no website" pass rate per US metro is the operational number.
- Thryv's 100K clients at $373/mo is the proof that owners pay $300-$400/month for a bundled site + listings + CRM when it is sold to them; the Mewayz $124 median is what they pay when they self-serve. The founder's price should sit between: sold, not self-served, but priced below the reseller tier.
- The AI-builder market ($3.2B) is tiny relative to Wix alone ($2B revenue), so "AI builds your site" is a feature, not a market; it does not change who the founder competes with.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative US "SMB web services" market-size figure was found; only vendor reports and the AI-builder subsegment. IDC/Gartner SMB IT spend figures were not reachable.
- The Mewayz report is self-published by a software vendor; sample and method could not be verified.

## Key question 2: Who are the competitors in each category, with 2026 pricing, positioning, strengths, weaknesses and traction?

### Takeaway
Every category except the local resellers is cheaper than the founder, and every category except freelancers is better capitalized; the founder's only unoccupied position is "done-for-you, month-to-month, owner owns everything, integrations set up for you" at $99-$199/mo, which no DIY builder offers and which the resellers offer only with contracts and a 1-star reputation.

### Inferences
- Price ladder the owner sees today: $0-$39 DIY, $10-$42 AI builder, $29-$299 vertical ops tool, $300-$500 reseller bundle, $3,000-$5,000 freelancer one-off. A $99-$199/mo done-for-you plan with a $0-$500 setup lands exactly in the empty rung between "do it yourself" and "sign a reseller contract".
- The vertical players' websites are loss leaders attached to the thing they monetize (orders, jobs, appointments). None of them wants to be a web agency; all of them expose APIs or OAuth (per the sibling integration notes). They are app-menu partners, and the founder's site becomes the owner-controlled front door that posts into them.
- Agency platforms (Duda $149, GHL $497) prove agencies will pay $150-$500/mo for a multi-tenant engine. The founder already owns that layer on Cloudflare at ~$5/mo, so the engine is a cost advantage and, later, a possible white-label revenue line.
- The AI app builders are the founder's future self-serve substrate; the Base44 acquisition shows Wix will bring prompt-to-site to its 250M-user base (unverified: Wix registered-user count, 2024), so self-serve is not a durable differentiator for a solo founder. Done-for-you plus integrations plus relationship is.
- Durable's "6 million sites, revenue material, undisclosed" and B12's six-year funding gap are evidence that AI-generated presence sites alone do not produce a venture-scale business; the money in SMB web is in the managed bundle (Thryv $373 ARPU) or the vertical workflow (Owner $100M ARR).

## Key question 3: What evidence exists of churn or dissatisfaction with the local resellers (Thryv, Hibu, Townsquare, LocalIQ)?

### Takeaway
The dissatisfaction evidence is consistent and specific (contract lock-in, cancellation fees, oversold features, unresponsive support, outcomes not delivered) but mostly from review sites and competitor-hosted pages; Thryv's own filings show 94% net revenue retention and a deliberate exit from marketing services, which confirms the legacy bundle is shrinking fast (-32.6% in 2025) and that the SaaS base it is migrating to is not fully sticky.

### Cited Findings
- Thryv Trustpilot: 73% of reviews are one star; reviewers report it is "nearly impossible" to get a refund or cancel a contract, a $10,000 cancellation fee after a family emergency, outages and slow systems, a plan sold with five users reduced to three after signing, and sales staff selling features that do not exist — [B12 Orchestra Thryv review roundup](https://orchestra.b12.io/reviews/thryv/) (hosted by competitor B12; treat as hostile aggregation), [Trustpilot Thryv](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/thryv.com), [Xero App Store Thryv reviews](https://apps.xero.com/us/app/thryv/reviews)
- Thryv financials: Marketing Services revenue $324.0M in 2025, down 32.6%, "intentionally winding down" with a target exit by 2028; NRR 94% for established SaaS customers; $20.4M of 2025 SaaS growth came from converting marketing-services clients — [Thryv 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1556739/000155673926000013/thry-20251231.htm), [MarketChameleon](https://marketchameleon.com/articles/b/2026/2/27/thryv-saas-growth-ai-platform-2025-results)
- Hibu (as RevLocal Hibu): 8 BBB complaints in the last three years, 3 closed in the last 12 months — [BBB](https://nginx-legacy.prod.bbb.org/us/oh/westerville/profile/digital-marketing/revlocal-hibu-0302-70083753/complaints)
- Townsquare Interactive: customers report being promised "lots of clients" that did not come at a high price, web standing that "dropped continually", tasks "half done if at all", 30-90 day cancellation notice and costs above the advertised $300-$500 range — [Trustpilot Townsquare (29 reviews)](https://se.trustpilot.com/review/townsquareinteractive.com), [Townsquare alternatives page](https://risacare-demo.duckdns.org/townsquare-alternatives) (low authority)
- LocalIQ: users report the company is non-responsive, will not call back, and "lied" about the number of leads — [G2 LocalIQ](https://g2.com/products/localiq/pricing)
- Home-service owners with websites are "often unhappy with their site's appearance, functionality and/or cost" (Housecall Pro) — sibling `business_types_ranking.md` KQ2, citing [PM Magazine](https://pmmag.com/articles/102211-housecall-pro-helps-home-service-companies-build-free-professional-websites)

### Inferences
- A 94% NRR on a $373 ARPU base means Thryv loses roughly 6% of revenue per year net of expansion on its "established" cohort; combined with the 32.6% marketing-services decline, several hundred million dollars a year of SMB spend is leaving the legacy reseller model and looking for a home. The owners who leave are precisely the founder's secondary segment ("has a site on an older or more expensive platform").
- The complaint pattern (contracts, fees, oversold features, no support) writes the founder's positioning for free: month-to-month, owner owns domain/content/Stripe account, written scope, a named human. This should be on the pricing page, not just in the pitch.
- No FTC action against Thryv, Hibu, Townsquare or LocalIQ surfaced in search; the dissatisfaction is a reputational, not a regulatory, signal.

### Gaps
- Trustpilot review counts and TrustScores for Thryv and Townsquare could not be read (blocked); only the 73% one-star share (via B12's page) and "29 reviews" (Townsquare) were visible.
- No FTC or state AG complaint data was found; FTC and BBB sites were not fetchable.
- Hibu US corporate financials are private; no revenue or client count was found. (unverified) Hibu acquired RevLocal in 2024, which is why BBB lists them together.

## Key question 4: Where do "done-for-you + integrations + managed service" players sit, and how are they doing?

### Takeaway
The winners in managed SMB web are either vertical and workflow-owning (Owner.com, $100M+ ARR, $2.3B valuation, month-to-month at $249-$499) or scale resellers migrating to SaaS (Thryv, $461M SaaS revenue at 16% EBITDA margin); the horizontal AI-site players (Durable, B12) have volume without disclosed revenue, and the reviews/messaging layer (Podium) has gone quote-only with an AI-agent upsell.

### Cited Findings
- Owner.com: $240M Series D led by Goldman Sachs at $2.3B valuation, ARR above $100M, >$1B restaurant sales expected in 2026; pricing $249 + 5% or $499 flat, month-to-month — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/owner-raises-240-million-at-2-3-billion-valuation-as-arr-tops-100-million/), [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/adam-guild-owner-240m-series-d-ai-restaurants), [Sacra Owner](https://sacra.com/c/owner); May 2025 $1B valuation — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/restaurant-tech-startup-owner-com-hits-1-billion-valuation)
- Thryv: SaaS adjusted EBITDA margin 16.0% in 2025 (from 12.0%), 100K SaaS clients, ARPU $373, 69% of SaaS revenue from >$400 MRR clients; net debt $265M, 1.9x leverage — [MarketChameleon](https://marketchameleon.com/articles/b/2026/2/27/thryv-saas-growth-ai-platform-2025-results), [Quartr Q3 2025](https://quartr.com/events/thryv-holdings-inc-thry-q3-2025_3eRDGOdN), [Thryv 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1556739/000155673926000013/thry-20251231.htm)
- Durable: 6M+ generated sites, revenue "material" but undisclosed, $18M CAD raise aimed at service businesses running "on autopilot" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2640709), [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/?p=367519)
- B12: $28.1M total raised, no round since March 2020, 150,000+ businesses served — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/b12/financials), [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/startups/b12)
- Podium: custom pricing only as of June 2026; AI Employee add-on sold with a dedicated CSM and system-of-record integration — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/dental-software/podium-dental/), [Podium AI](https://www.podium.com/ai/)
- Housecall Pro and Jobber both now bundle free website builders for home-service firms — sibling `business_types_ranking.md`, citing [PHCP Pros](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/9644-housecall-pro-offers-website-buider) and [HVAC Insider](https://hvacinsider.com/jobber-empowers-home-service-pros-to-attract-more-customers-and-grow-smarter-with-new-digital-marketing-tools/)

### Inferences
- Owner.com is the proof that "done-for-you site + ordering + marketing automation + integrations, sold by humans, no contract" works at $249-$499/mo, but only because it owns the order flow. The founder cannot copy the price in restaurants without ordering; the model transfers to retail (Stripe + ShipStation + Mailchimp, the founder's existing stack) and services (quote/booking posting into Jobber/Housecall Pro).
- Thryv's 16% SaaS EBITDA margin with a large sales force versus the founder's near-zero COGS on Cloudflare suggests a solo operator can be profitable at a fraction of Thryv's ARPU, provided onboarding is templated (the "one engine" rule in the founder's vision).
- Durable and B12 show the horizontal, self-serve, AI-site business is a volume game with weak monetization; the founder's self-serve ambition should remain a long-term efficiency layer, not the business.
- Podium going quote-only and pushing an "AI Employee" signals the reviews/messaging layer is moving upmarket; a Google Business Profile + review-request integration in the founder's app menu covers the small-owner version of that need (see sibling marketing/comms integration note).

### Gaps

- Owner.com's restaurant count was not found in the search extracts.

## Key question 5: Who is the founder really competing with for each customer type?

### Takeaway
The real competitor changes by archetype: for no-website restaurants it is free (Square Online, DoorDash Storefront, Facebook) and Owner.com's sales reps; for no-website service businesses it is the free sites inside Jobber/Housecall Pro and the Townsquare/Thryv phone call; for a retailer leaving Shopify it is Shopify's own inertia plus Wix/Squarespace; for an auto parts store wanting inventory online it is Square for Retail/Lightspeed templates and a local dev shop quote.

### Cited Findings
- No-website restaurant: incumbents from $0/mo (Square Online Free, DoorDash Storefront) to $119-$499/mo plus 2.9-3.5% processing (ChowNow, Owner.com, Toast, Popmenu) and Slice at 5% of sales; 72% of restaurants already have a site — sibling `business_model_and_market.md` KQ2 and KQ8; [GetSauce Owner](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees); [Slice pricing](https://slice.com/pricing)
- No-website service business: >40% of home-service businesses have no website (Housecall Pro) and >50% of small businesses (Jobber), and both vendors give away a website builder inside $29-$299/mo plans; Townsquare sells the same owner a $300-$500/mo bundle by phone — sibling `business_types_ranking.md` KQ1-2; [ContractorToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/reports/contractor-software-pricing-2026/); [Townsquare alternatives](https://risacare-demo.duckdns.org/townsquare-alternatives)
- Retailer switching from Shopify: Shopify Basic ~$39/mo, Wix Core $29, Squarespace Core $36 (annual $23), Square Online free; merchant complaints center on removed phone support, fee increases and stacked BNPL fees — [BlackBeltCommerce](https://www.blackbeltcommerce.com/compare-shopify-vs-wix-vs-squarespace-find-your-best-fit/), [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-phone-support-has-been-removed/227138?page=2), [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/shopify-complaints-mount-from-online-retailers-and-consumers-110419.html); Shopify OAuth import is self-serve for the founder — sibling `integrations_payments_commerce_accounting.md` KQ1
- Auto parts store wanting inventory online: Square for Retail/Square POS $0-$149/mo with a free online store, Lightspeed $89-$289/mo with vendor catalogs and multi-location inventory, Shopify POS Pro +$89; custom builds from boutique agencies $8,000-$50,000 and up — [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-pos-systems), [NerdWallet Lightspeed](https://www.nerdwallet.com/reviews/small-business/lightspeed-retail-pos), [ParallelHQ](https://parallelhq.com/blog/website-design-prices); Lightspeed X-Series and Square OAuth are self-serve up to connection caps — sibling `integrations_payments_commerce_accounting.md` KQ1-2
- Salon/barber (a top primary type per sibling ranking): GlossGenius $24, Vagaro $30/calendar, Booksy marketplace with $0 reserve fee; hair salons have the lowest website adoption (62.6%) — [Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/compare/glossgenius-vs-vagaro/), [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/small-business-statistics)

### Inferences
- Archetype 1, no-website restaurant: do not compete with Owner.com or Toast on ordering. Sell a $99-$149/mo menu-and-brand site that links to the ordering system the restaurant already has (Square, Toast, DoorDash Storefront) and owns the email list; the "food" engine variant is a DoorDash/Toast/Square integration, not an ordering engine. Expect the owner to compare against "free", so the pitch is photos, Google ranking, direct-order links that cut 15-30% marketplace commission (sibling KQ4 fee math).
- Archetype 2, no-website service business: the strongest lane. The competitor is a free-but-ugly Jobber/Housecall Pro site or a reseller contract. Win with a better front end whose quote/booking form posts into Jobber/Housecall Pro via the app menu, month-to-month, at $99-$199/mo, with the reseller horror stories as the contrast.
- Archetype 3, retailer leaving Shopify: the competitor is inertia. Win with a migration (OAuth product import, Stripe + ShipStation + Mailchimp pre-wired), 0% take rate, and a human on the phone, priced at or below Shopify Basic plus its app stack ($29-$49/mo per sibling KQ8) with a $199-$499 migration fee.
- Archetype 4, auto parts/hardware with legacy inventory: the competitor is "do nothing" or a Lightspeed/Square template that cannot do fitment or sync the legacy system. This is the $40k-$100k custom tier; the engine supplies storefront, cart, Stripe and ShipStation, and the custom spend goes into the inventory sync and vehicle-lookup data. Lightspeed at $89-$289/mo is also an integration target, not only a rival.
- Across all four, the founder is never the cheapest option; the only defensible claim is "it is done, it is wired to your tools, you own it, you can leave".

### Gaps
- Share of restaurants on each ordering platform, share of home-service firms using the free Jobber/HCP sites, and the number of Shopify merchants under $100k GMV who churn yearly were not found (sibling notes record the same gap).
- Auto-parts-specific competitors (eBay Motors fitment tooling, PartsTech, Epicor catalogs, MyPartsPlus-class turnkey parts sites) were not researched in this session. (unverified) Turnkey parts e-commerce vendors existed at $300-$1,500/mo in 2023.

## Key question 6: How does the founder differentiate (differentiation thesis)?

### Takeaway
The founder's position is the empty rung between DIY and the reseller contract: a done-for-you site on one reusable engine, wired to the tools the owner already pays for through an OAuth app menu, month-to-month, with the owner owning domain, data and Stripe account, at $99-$199/mo for presence and $29-$49/mo for storefronts, scaling to custom inventory builds where templates stop.

### Cited Findings
- Reseller tier charges $300-$500/mo under 30-90 day notice contracts with widespread complaints — [Townsquare alternatives](https://risacare-demo.duckdns.org/townsquare-alternatives), [Thryv reviews roundup](https://orchestra.b12.io/reviews/thryv/)
- Thryv's 100K clients pay $373/mo on average and 69% pay >$400/mo — [MarketChameleon](https://marketchameleon.com/articles/b/2026/2/27/thryv-saas-growth-ai-platform-2025-results)
- Owner.com's month-to-month, no-contract $249-$499 pricing is the model that reached $100M ARR — [GetSauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees), [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/owner-raises-240-million-at-2-3-billion-valuation-as-arr-tops-100-million/)
- 78% of SMBs prefer integrated platforms; $19-$49 is the dominant price point — [Mewayz](https://mewayz.com/blog/the-state-of-small-business-software-in-2026-annual-report)
- Agencies pay $149-$497/mo for white-label engines; the founder's Cloudflare cost is ~$5/mo per account — sibling `business_model_and_market.md` KQ1; [Duda pricing](https://www.joinsecret.com/duda/pricing), [GHL pricing](https://ghlexperts.com/gohighlevel-plans-pricing)
- Self-serve OAuth is available today for Stripe, Square, Shopify, Lightspeed X-Series, QuickBooks, Xero, Mailchimp; API-key fallback for ShipStation, WooCommerce, PayPal non-partner — sibling `integrations_payments_commerce_accounting.md` KQ1-3

### Inferences
- Thesis in one line: "Thryv's outcome at Wix's price, without the contract." Concretely: (1) done-for-you, remote, in days; (2) the integrations are the product, offered as an app menu with OAuth so the owner never sees an API key; (3) month-to-month and owner-owned (domain, content export, their own Stripe account) as the explicit answer to reseller lock-in; (4) one engine with four variants so unit economics approach Duda/GHL-style margins at a solo founder's scale; (5) a custom tier ($40k-$100k) for inventory-heavy retailers that no template or vertical SaaS serves, using the same engine as the storefront.
- Against DIY and AI builders, the differentiator is not technology but completion: the owner's site gets built, photographed, wired and kept current by someone accountable. Against verticals, the differentiator is neutrality: the founder's front door works with whichever Jobber/Square/Toast the owner already uses and survives a switch between them.
- The weakest point of the thesis is restaurants (ordering is the product there) and the strongest are home services, salons and independent retail/makers, which matches the sibling ranking.
- Durability risk: Wix (Base44), GoDaddy (Airo) and Hostinger will make AI-generated sites free; the founder's moat must be the integration layer, the relationship, and the custom tier, never the site generator.

### Gaps
- No competitor was found that explicitly combines done-for-you build, an owner-facing OAuth integration menu, month-to-month pricing and a custom-software tier; that absence is a positioning opportunity but could also mean the bundle is hard to sell; it is unproven either way.
- Willingness-to-pay for "owner owns everything, no contract" versus a cheaper locked contract was not measured by any source found.
