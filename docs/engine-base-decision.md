## 1. Bottom line

Build the engine from HallForge, and port the three things CatholicJoe does better (commerce reconciliation, ShipStation fulfillment, the email outbox) into it as modules. HallForge already contains, finished and in production, roughly 60–65% of what `docs/engine-architecture.md` planned to build from scratch: hostname tenancy on Cloudflare Workers, a capability and role model, plans and entitlements with Stripe Billing, a strict Zod block document with an isomorphic compiler, immutable published snapshots, review and conflict handling, autosave with recovery, a tested and accessible dashboard, media on R2, a calendar, a hardened contact form, a platform-operator surface, and a harness of about 2,070 Vitest cases and 1,575 pgTAP assertions (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §7, bottom line; `hallforge_readiness_docs_business.md` "Net"). CatholicJoe has none of that and about 16 test files; what it has that HallForge lacks is a few thousand lines of excellent, self-contained commerce and fulfillment code that port into a Postgres module far more cheaply than HallForge's 32,000 lines of SQL and 150,000 lines of TypeScript would port into Astro and D1. The honest price of this choice is architectural: you give up the engine document's one-Worker-and-one-D1-per-tenant model and accept Next.js on OpenNext over a shared Supabase Postgres with row-level security, which costs about $25–$35 a month more at small scale, concentrates blast radius, and has no per-tenant export primitive today. Those are real but fixable; a second codebase for a solo founder supporting a family is not. Under this choice "finishing HallForge" (billing live, tier gates on, custom domains, registration and checkout) and "building the engine" are the same work order, and the four Knights councils keep running on the same deploy throughout.

## 4. The stack question

### 4.1 Next.js on OpenNext on Workers versus Astro on Workers

What the notes establish. HallForge runs Next 16.2.10 and React 19.2.7 as one Worker through `@opennextjs/cloudflare` 1.20.2 with `nodejs_compat`, static assets and three R2 bindings (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §1). The adapter lags Next's conventions: the file is still `middleware.ts` on the edge runtime because OpenNext did not support the Node-runtime `proxy.ts` (`src/middleware.ts:L336-L340`), Node built-ins crash that boundary (`node:net` caused a production outage; `ip.ts` reimplements `isIP`), and `sharp` is denied so image variants are produced in the browser (`hallforge_readiness_docs_business.md` §2 "Runtime constraints"). ISR is configured but unused: the live public route is `force-dynamic` with `revalidate = 0`, so every tenant page render performs several Supabase RPCs and the R2 incremental cache binding sits idle (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §5). Two production-only defects that CI could not see are why the deploy job runs a live smoke check (`docs/launch/2026-08-20-first-end-to-end-verification.md:L14-L30`).

CatholicJoe's Astro build has none of those constraints: `imageService: 'compile'` makes images free, the Worker is a thin handler, and the Cloudflare note records "no meaningful cold start for a typical Astro SSR bundle" (`cloudflare_architecture.md` Q11). The hard limits that apply to both are a 64 MiB uncompressed Worker, 128 MB per isolate, 30 s default CPU per request and 6 simultaneous outbound connections (`cloudflare_architecture.md` Q11; Workers limits).

What the notes do not establish: the size of HallForge's OpenNext bundle, its CPU-ms per request, or its cold start. The infra note says "no doc records Worker bundle-size or CPU-time limits as a measured concern" and CI builds the bundle without asserting its size (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §5). So: Next on OpenNext is heavier, more constrained and less mature on Workers than Astro, it is in production serving four councils, and nobody has measured by how much. The first task under any HallForge option is to run `cf:build`, record bundle size and CPU-ms, and cache public pages (`s-maxage` with purge-on-publish, or the published snapshot in the R2 incremental cache) so storefront traffic is not several Postgres round-trips per view.

Why it is acceptable anyway: the public site's only client islands are the enquiry form, the video embed and a motion wrapper (`hallforge_content_blocks_editor.md` §3 "Renderer"), so the heavy React is in the dashboard, where it earns its keep: a 7,879-line editor whose live preview runs the public renderer, TipTap, a media picker with stock search, autosave with IndexedDB recovery, axe gates (`hallforge_content_blocks_editor.md` §2). The engine document's Astro admin would have grown CatholicJoe's four vanilla-TS pages into server-rendered tables (`engine-architecture.md` §8.2), a from-scratch admin.

### 4.2 Supabase Postgres shared with RLS versus D1 per tenant

Cost, with the numbers the notes have. HallForge's fixed floor is about $31 a month (Supabase Pro $25, Workers Paid $5, domain, Resend free, R2 pennies), $51 once Resend Pro is needed; the Path D model gives $40 a month at 5 orgs, $358 at 250 and $8,783 at 17,000, with marginal cost per org of $0.03–$0.80 (`hallforge_readiness_docs_business.md` §2; `docs/decisions/2026-08-18-platform-cost-paths.md:L24-L33`). The engine document's D1 model gives $5 at 10 sites, $5–10 at 50 and $25–50 at 200 (`cloudflare_architecture.md` Q8). So the Supabase path costs roughly $35–45 more per month at 50 tenants and $250–300 more at 200. Against a blended ARPU of $129–179 (`viability_solo_founder.md` Q10), 200 tenants is $26–36k MRR and the difference is about one percent of revenue. The real cost risk is Supabase's ladder: PITR and larger compute near 250 orgs, Team at $599 at national scale, with a Neon plus Better Auth exit already written (`platform-cost-paths.md:L45-L51`). Cost does not decide this question.

Handoff and export. D1 per tenant makes a tenant "a D1 export plus a pinned template", so an exit is a CNAME change (`engine-architecture.md` §10). HallForge has no export, archive or delete-organization primitive; `people.export` is a capability name with no implementation (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §3). The same note says why it is tractable: every row carries `organization_id`, media is keyed `org/<id>/…` in R2, the published site is immutable JSON, so an `export_organization(p_org)` function returning JSONB plus an R2 prefix copy plus a static render of each snapshot is "~1 migration + pgTAP suite". You cannot give a client "their database"; you can give them their data, media and a static site, which for the managed tier the strategy report makes the default (Model 1) is the exit that matters.

Blast radius. A bad migration on shared Postgres hits every tenant; on D1 it hits one (`cloudflare_architecture.md` Q4). HallForge's mitigation is unusually strong: 1,575 pgTAP assertions on a fresh Supabase every push, including a `pg_proc` scan proving every function is revoked and granted correctly, forced RLS with no browser privileges, and composite foreign keys that stop a child pointing at another tenant's parent (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §3). It has one hole: `production-database` is not a dependency of `cloudflare-deploy`, so a migration can ship without pgTAP gating it (`hallforge_platform_infra.md` §4 Gaps). That is a one-line CI fix and the first commit.

The 50,000-database model. D1 offers 50,000 databases per account at 10 GB each (`cloudflare_architecture.md` Q4), and the engine document builds a fleet on it: a Worker and D1 per tenant, a router, KV hostname maps, `deploy_jobs` as Workflows, template pinning, migration waves, a central scheduler before the 250-cron cap, Workers for Platforms near 400 Workers (`engine-architecture.md` §4.12, §10). HallForge needs none of it: one deploy, one migration stream, one cron, one wildcard route, no 500-Worker cap, no 128-secrets-per-Worker limit. The engine document's phase 4 (10–14 days) and most of its fleet design disappear; what you lose is per-tenant CPU caps and client-supplied code, which the vision does not need. Postgres also removes two D1 limits the engine document designed around for the auto-parts store: FTS5 blocks export, and 100 bound parameters and 100 columns per query (`cloudflare_architecture.md` Q4; `engine-architecture.md` §13 Q7).

Vendor lock-in. HallForge's database decision limits it deliberately: Supabase "as infrastructure, not as the application architecture", identity behind an interface, portable SQL, and documented triggers for a Neon plus Better Auth exit (`docs/decisions/2026-07-15-database-platform.md:L129-L177`). The real coupling is Supabase Auth and 276 `SECURITY DEFINER` functions of plpgsql, which any Postgres host runs.

### 4.3 tRPC plus a React admin versus an Astro SSR admin

HallForge's admin is not tRPC. The router is `{}` by design and the API is 30 `"use server"` files and 13 route handlers calling RPCs, while the tRPC guard middlewares (`authenticatedProcedure`, `organizationProcedure`, `capabilityProcedure`, `featureProcedure`) are written and unused (`src/server/root.ts:L1-L19`; `src/server/trpc/index.ts:L103-L207`). So the choice is not "tRPC or Astro" but "keep the server-action pattern and decide what tRPC is for". The recommendation is to adopt tRPC for exactly one thing: the §5.5 tool router. A typed, introspectable procedure set (`get_page`, `insert_block`, `update_block`, `move_block`, `publish_draft`, `set_hours`, `get_connect_url`) built over the existing repository functions, gated by the middlewares that already exist, gives the editor, provisioning scripts and the future AI one surface, which is the engine document's "all mutation goes through one typed API" principle (`engine-architecture.md` §2). If the founder does not want tRPC at all, the same router can be plain Zod-validated route handlers; the dependency is not the point, the single typed surface is.

An Astro SSR admin would be lighter, but it does not exist: CatholicJoe's admin is four pages and a 947-line `innerHTML` client the teardown says needs a framework before multiplying screens (`reference_repo_teardown.md` Q5 Inferences); HallForge's is 37 pages with tests.

### 4.4 What each choice means for the AI builder

The engine document's §11 preconditions are: a strict, versioned, expressive block schema; all mutations through the typed tool set the human editor uses; a signed preview URL per revision; integrations connected only by URL handoff; `audit_events` capturing before and after revisions; budgets as settings; presets as good defaults. HallForge has the strict schema (`z.discriminatedUnion`, every block `.strict()`, 1,000-node rich text cap, link safety), optimistic concurrency on save, revisions with `created_by`, an `audit_events` table and a template registry. It lacks block-level mutations, an `actor_kind` of `user|platform|ai`, a shareable signed preview, and `migrate.ts` (`hallforge_content_blocks_editor.md` §1 Gaps). Those are 3–5 days on top of an existing document model. Under the Astro option every one of them is built from nothing in phase 2 and then again in phase 10. Under the hybrid, the AI edits in the control plane and the result is rendered by a different codebase, which means the model's preview and the public page can disagree. The editor direction (click-to-edit, drag-to-arrange, Puck as a candidate, `docs/decisions/2026-09-01-direct-manipulation-editors.md:L13-L45`) is independent of this decision and is the same work in any option.

### 4.5 What each choice means for the integrations framework

The engine document's framework (`connections`, `webhook_events`, `provider_jobs`, `connection_health`, capability interfaces, an OAuth broker, three UI patterns, an Apps page, hourly health) is new work in every option; the notes put it at 10–14 days (`engine-architecture.md` §12 phase 3). The difference is where secrets live and how webhooks route. The engine document stores per-tenant secrets as Worker secrets on the tenant's own Worker and routes Stripe Connect and Square webhooks by external account id (`engine-architecture.md` §7.5–7.6). In a shared Worker that design is unavailable, and HallForge's answer is already built: tokens sealed with AES-256-GCM under a versioned master key with the organization id as authenticated data (`056:L20-L44`), and a Connect webhook that ingests by account before acknowledging (`src/server/payments/orchestration/README.md:L31-L50`). Rotating OAuth tokens (Shopify, QuickBooks) fit the sealed-credential store naturally; the engine document had to invent `connection_tokens` for the same purpose. Nango remains optional for the long tail in both.

## 6. Recommendation

Option A, with three conditions and one rule.

The reasoning. The engine document's 65–91 days bought a presence preset, a from-scratch admin, a from-scratch block pipeline and a fleet; it did not include an editor with live preview, a media library, plans and entitlements, Stripe Billing, a calendar, a contact inbox, invitations with an owner guard or 3,600 tests, and it left HallForge as a second codebase. Option A reaches a first release in the same 65–94 days with all of those included, because what HallForge has not done (a scheduler, reachable checkout, custom domains, real email, an export, Connect onboarding) is what the business engine needs, and what HallForge has done (tenancy, access, billing, publishing, media) is what the engine document would have spent its first thirty days rebuilding. HallForge is a multi-tenant platform whose only product problem is that every noun says council.

The three conditions. First, measure before committing further: run the OpenNext build, record bundle size and CPU-ms per public request, and put a cache in front of public pages in the first week; if the numbers are bad enough that a storefront's public traffic cannot be served from one Worker, the published-snapshot contract keeps a renderer swap possible. Second, gate deploys on pgTAP and ship `export_organization()` before the first paying business, so the blast-radius and handoff objections to a shared database are answered in code rather than argued. Third, treat the sealed-credential store, the Connect webhook ingest and the leased-worker tables as the integrations framework's foundation rather than building a parallel one, because they are the correct design for a shared Worker and they are tested.

The rule: everything Knights-specific is gated by `organization_type_key = 'kofc'` and nothing is deleted. Offices, programs, recognitions, prayer, changeover, volunteers, the print newsletter and the design studio stay exactly as they are for councils and simply do not appear for a business. The orgtype layer was built for this ("no module outside `compliance` may hard-code a vertical's nouns", `src/modules/orgtype/index.ts:L7-L8`); the product layer never adopted it, and A1 is where it finally does.

What "finishing HallForge" means under option A. The founder's own ordering is billing live, tier gates on, custom domains, then the member and compliance wedge (`phase-roadmap.md:L416-L424`). The first three are A0, A1's gating pass and A8. Registration and ticketing, a 503 stub today, becomes the first consumer of A4's checkout: one payments rail that sells a fish-fry ticket and a box of brake pads through the same `payment_orders`, Connect account and leased workers. The council store (roadmap 11b, "dials only") is A4's catalog under a council org type. Newsletter email (roadmap 12) is A6's outbox plus a digest template. Only the compliance wedge (Form 1728, Star Council) is Knights-only and stays on its own schedule. Every other HallForge roadmap item is on the business engine's critical path, and the Knights get them sooner because a paying business funds them.

How the councils keep working. All four production councils are on Free with nothing recorded outside Free except the pilot's three studio designs (`docs/decisions/2026-09-02-plan-tiers-presence-communication-fundraising.md:L83`), so exposure is small. Migrations stay additive; existing rows keep `organization_type_key = 'kofc'` by default (`010:L107-L109`); the `council_owner` to `owner` rename is one migration updating `role_presets`, the bootstrap literal and the dashboard constant together, verified by the 058 pgTAP suite; council-only lanes are hidden for businesses, never removed; deploy order stays migration first, then Worker (`README.md:L85-L95`), with the smoke check expecting a council subdomain to return 200. The vocabulary scrub goes one lane at a time behind the tests and axe gates. A published council page cannot regress from a scrub, because it is served from an immutable snapshot; only the dashboard can break, and that is where tests are densest (42 files under `app/(dashboard)`, 24 under `components/dashboard`, `hallforge_readiness_docs_business.md` §5).

What this costs the vision. "Link each customer to its Cloudflare Worker site" (`founder_vision.md`) becomes "link each customer to its organization and hostname". Owners still never handle API keys; the AI builder gets a better substrate sooner. The one loss is a client walking away with a Worker and database of their own; they walk away with their data, their media and a static copy of their site, and the strategy report already made the managed tier the default.

## 7. Open questions

1. Are the four councils live with real visitors and officers signing in today, and which migrations are applied in production? The last runbook that states it says 001–034 aligned and 055 unpushed (`hallforge_readiness_docs_business.md` §1 Gaps); 056–060 are unknown. The answer sets how careful A1 must be.
2. Is Cloudflare the only target? `vercel.json` disables deployments and the roadmap says Vercel retired, but `www` and apex DNS still point at Vercel behind Worker Routes (`wrangler.jsonc:L7-L11`). Confirm there is no Vercel bill and move the DNS.
3. What is the actual Supabase bill (Free or Pro at $25), and who owns the Supabase organization? Every cost figure in the notes is modelled, not invoiced (`hallforge_readiness_docs_business.md` §2 Gaps).
4. How attached are you to tRPC? The router is empty; the recommendation is to use it for the tool surface only, or delete `@trpc/*`, `@tanstack/react-query` and `superjson` and use route handlers.
5. What are the OpenNext bundle size and CPU-ms per public request? Nothing in the repo measures them; it is the first task.
6. Is the Stripe account still unactivated (`details_submitted` false) with no `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` on the Worker (`billing-go-live.md:L14-L18`)? Billing go-live is an afternoon once it is.
7. Was the trademark attorney's opinion on the neutral-host posture obtained before charging money (`go-no-go-verdict.md:L39`)? Nothing records it, and a business engine under the HallForge name raises the same question twice.
8. Does the business engine ship under the HallForge brand or the new name (`naming.md`)? One Worker can serve both brands by adding a second platform host and tenant root to the host classifier; the decision affects the wildcard zone, email sender and landing page, not the code.
9. Does CatholicJoe become a tenant of the engine (a `retail-catalog` org with a one-SKU catalog and its own ShipStation key), stay standalone, or both for a while? Making it a tenant is the honest test of A4 and A5.
10. Does the family auto-parts store run a POS (Epicor Eagle, Vision, a jobber system) and can its distributor supply ACES/PIES files? That decides whether the custom tier is a catalog import or a sync, and VCdb licensing must be in the store's name (`engine-architecture.md` §13 Q10).
11. Do you want the Konva design studio and the print newsletter in the business product at all, or are they council-only lanes? The recommendation is council-only until a business asks.
12. Are you willing to enforce MFA for owners before the first business takes money? The claims carry `aal` and nothing checks it; the roadmap calls it the largest security gap.
13. Puck or a custom direct-manipulation shell for the editor's next step, and when? It is the same work in any option, and it should not precede the first paying business.
14. Who are the first two local businesses, and is one of them the family store? The thirty-day exit criterion needs a real tenant with real hours and a real address.
